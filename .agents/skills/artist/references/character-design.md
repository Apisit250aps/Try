# Character design and construction

Use for original characters, costume, expression/pose studies and model sheets.
[ScreenSkills](../research/sources.md#a03) supports research, option development,
medium-aware design and approved multi-view sheets. This guide turns those duties
into local decision tests. Continuity records live in [consistency.md](consistency.md).

## Design a recognizable visual system

Translate supplied role, behavior and world facts into construction choices.
Start with the cast relationship: relative height, mass distribution, posture,
hair/head silhouette, costume profile and negative spaces. Test each design as
flat masses and at target size. If two characters differ only in hue, add a
non-color distinction when individual recognition matters.

Shape language can organize a design, but cannot diagnose personality, morality,
gender or culture. The [museum guide](../research/sources.md#a07) itself describes
exceptions and mixed shapes. Example: a triangular cape may communicate forward
motion for a rescuer; it need not label a villain. Counterexample: making every
antagonist angular and every hero round weakens range and can contradict the brief.
Explore differences in proportion and mass before choosing surface decoration.

## Anatomy and stylization

Build gesture and connected volumes before contour polish: head, ribcage, pelvis,
limbs and contact points. [Proko's construction lesson](../research/sources.md#a08)
uses simple forms to preserve solidity. Use observed anatomy appropriate to the
subject; do not impose one standard human ratio on children, stylized figures,
animals or different body types.

Local tests: identify support or intended imbalance, joint connections, plausible
reach and hand-object contact. For an oversized chibi head, decide neck support,
hand simplification and limb range consistently. Stylization changes proportions
and omission; it should preserve the intended action. An impossible elbow is not
explained away by calling the figure cute. Conversely, a deliberate graphic bend
within an approved squash/stretch language need not be corrected into realism.

When detail is constrained, simplify secondary fingers/seams before losing the
pose or identity landmarks. Distinguish drawing limitations from missing design
information. Seek reference for an unfamiliar grip/anatomy instead of adding
ornament to conceal it.

## Pose and expression

Judge meaning, silhouette, line of action and weight relationship from the
intended frame, informed by [Anthony Wong's lesson](../research/sources.md#a09).
A pose can be balanced yet fail to express the requested exertion. Check that
hands and gaze support the supplied action rather than merely making a striking
outline. Do not move a locked camera to rescue a pose without proposing it.

For expressions, keep the same head construction and feature landmarks, then
vary brow/eyelid tension, gaze, cheek compression, mouth angle and jaw opening as
the look permits. [Dana Boadway-Masson](../research/sources.md#a10) relates face
posing to body posing and camera-readable gaze. Use neutral plus relevant emotional
extremes when a reusable expression range is needed. These are performance design
choices, not universal scientific emotion codes.

Example: “focused, not hostile” can use a stable gaze, slight lid narrowing and
relaxed mouth. Lowered inward brows plus compressed lips may instead read angry;
compare at use size. Counterexample: widening the eyes, shrinking the jaw and
changing the nose for surprise produces another face rather than another
expression. Do not demand an open silhouette if an intentionally hidden hand or
restrained performance is essential to the brief.

## Costume and accessories

Take action, setting, period, resources and character facts from the brief. Design
layers, closures, seams, wear and prop attachment so the character can perform the
action. Costume communicates a person in context, rather than simply showcasing
fashion; the [Academy guide](../research/sources.md#a11) describes script-led
research and collaboration with Director and Cinematographer.

Local tests: check shoulder/hip clearance, sleeve/hand access, strap routing,
fastening sides and pocket/prop reach. Choose where to simplify construction for
the medium. Repeated tiny ornaments cost continuity effort and can disappear at
small scale. Record base materials, variant IDs and scene wear separately.
A wet coat is a state; a new permanent crest is a design revision. Do not invent
cultural rank, sacred markings or biography to justify a costume detail.

## Model sheets and turnarounds

1. Identify the downstream use: illustrator, animator, modeler or only a pitch.
   Agree on useful views and unresolved surfaces; a beauty shot cannot supply
   hidden construction. Do not label a concept sheet animation-ready by default.
2. Lock neutral scale/baseline, head/body ratios, landmarks, costume and material
   swatches. Choose front, needed side(s), back and three-quarter as useful.
   Use construction/orthographic views for measuring; label expressive perspective
   views separately. Show anatomical left/right explicitly for asymmetry.
3. Compare landmark heights and volume across views. Check jaw depth, ear position,
   hair mass, shoulder/pelvis relationship, strap paths and accessory attachment.
   A back view cannot be obtained by mirroring a front view.
4. Add expression/action samples only where they clarify allowed deformation,
   garment behavior or identity. For an unknown back design, make a labeled draft
   proposal and preserve the established front; do not fabricate approval.
5. Deliver actual sheets or explicitly labeled specifications, with view labels,
   scale information and unsolved details. Use [character-sheet.md](../templates/character-sheet.md).
   Image generation does not prove cross-view volume consistency; inspect each view.

For supplied identity art, use [image editing](image-guidelines.md) and attach the
actual reference. No seed, character name or repeated description guarantees
identity. Use the [review rubric](review-rubric.md) to distinguish design quality
from compliance and from owner selection.
