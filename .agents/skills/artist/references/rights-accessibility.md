# Reference use, provenance and accessible delivery

Use when sourcing references, preparing public/commercial assets, or delivering
information-bearing graphics. Keep the record proportional to actual use; an
original private sketch does not need a clearance dossier.

## Source roles and allowed use

Record the source/creator and URL or supplied path, reference role (identity,
style, pose, research, incorporated asset), license/permission evidence and any
known restriction. A search result, available download or attribution alone is
not permission to incorporate an image. Prefer describing an observed principle
and making an original design instead of tracing distinctive composition/details.
The skill's MIT license does not relicense external references or certify asset
ownership.

For material actually incorporated into delivery, check the terms for that use:
redistribution, modification, commercial use and required credit/change notices.
For example, [CC BY 4.0](../research/sources.md#a19) has attribution/notice conditions
and does not grant every kind of right; [CC BY-NC](../research/sources.md#a20)
restricts commercial use. Inspect the actual license/version and permission
record rather than generalizing from “Creative Commons.” If the intended use is
unresolved, identify the specific dependency and offer an original replacement
or leave release pending; continue unaffected design. Do not announce legal
clearance or invent jurisdiction-specific conclusions.

For recognizable people, culturally specific/sacred elements, brands or licensed
characters, identify the relevant project permission/review requirement when the
brief makes it material. Apply the collection's generic-original design rules in
[stock mode](stock-guidelines.md). Do not substitute a story-critical object
silently. Research sources are not endorsements and their sample characters are
not assets supplied for copying.

## Provenance and revisions

For generated/edited production assets retain actual source paths/results,
reference roles, tool/model and settings if exposed, prompt/edit instructions,
date/version, changes and review state. If the tool does not expose a seed or
model version, say unknown; do not invent them. Preserve available content
credentials through the supported workflow when relevant. A manual record is
not a signed C2PA manifest. [C2PA 2.4](../research/sources.md#a21) explains provenance
integrity, not proof that depicted events are true or that rights are cleared.
Do not claim credentials were verified without a verification tool/result.

## Accessible use without changing the artwork's purpose

Determine context: decorative, informative, functional or complex.
[W3C's image tutorial](../research/sources.md#a17) differentiates text alternatives
by use. Offer concise essential-information text for an informative illustration,
function for a control, and a longer equivalent for complex diagrams when needed.
A decorative web image can use empty alt text. The publishing host decides how to
apply it; asset metadata alone does not prove an accessible page integration.

When color conveys identity, state or instructions, add shape, pattern, position
or labels rather than relying solely on hue; see [use of color](../research/sources.md#a18).
Test informative details at expected display size. A grayscale preview or color
vision simulation helps find defects but is not a complete accessibility audit.

For relevant web text/images of text, assess the applicable
[text contrast](../research/sources.md#a22) requirements (normally 4.5:1, 3:1 for
large text); meaningful graphical objects/controls have a separate
[non-text contrast](../research/sources.md#a23) criterion with exceptions.
Do not demand these ratios between every shade in a painting or certify WCAG
conformance from a single asset. Preserve the artistic brief while providing
usable labels/text equivalents at the publishing layer. If exact caption text
matters, favor editable text with verified glyphs over unreliable baked-in text.
