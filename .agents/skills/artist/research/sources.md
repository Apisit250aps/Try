# Artist source registry

Research date: **2026-11-25**. Each entry below was opened and its relevant public
text read on that date. “Read” does not mean a complete book, video or linked
course was consumed. Summaries are paraphrases; no external artwork, diagrams or
manuals are redistributed here. Section/page locators describe the inspected
material. These sources establish practices and constraints, not evidence that
this skill has acquired a professional's abilities. See [notes](notes.md) for
synthesis and source disagreements.

## A01

**Walt Disney Animation Studios — Visual Development.** Studio primary account.
[Source](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/visual-development/).
Read introduction, Making Moments and Creating Compelling Characters.
Supports script/director-guided look exploration and emotion conveyed in a visual
moment. Does not provide a quantitative design rubric or authorize copying its art.

## A02

**Walt Disney Animation Studios — Creative Development.** Studio primary account.
[Source](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/creative-development/).
Read Research, Building Teams, Providing Feedback. Supports research into culture,
history/science and collaborative iteration, including external voices. Does not
make one studio's staffing model mandatory for this project.

## A03

**ScreenSkills — Character designer (animation).** Industry skills body.
[Source](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/animation/pre-production/character-designer/).
Read duties, skills and collaborators. Supports anatomy/fashion research,
expression/pose design, alternatives, medium awareness and model sheets after
selection. A job profile identifies competencies; it is not a drawing textbook.

## A04

**ScreenSkills — Background designer (animation).** Industry skills body.
[Source](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/animation/pre-production/background-designer/).
Read duties and pipeline. Supports reusable background planning, foreground /
background interaction and style/color/light coherence. Notes that layout and
background duties can combine in small studios; roles are not universally rigid.

## A05

**J. Paul Getty Museum — Understanding Formal Analysis: Elements of Art (2011).**
Museum teaching guide, one-page PDF, read p.1.
[Source](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/building_lessons/elements_art.pdf).
Supports distinctions among line, flat shape, volume, negative space, hue/value /
intensity and texture. Its introductory pigment-primary claims are not used as
a universal model of digital color; use A16 for managed digital workflows.

## A06

**J. Paul Getty Museum — Understanding Formal Analysis: Principles of Design (2011).**
Museum teaching guide, one-page PDF, read p.1.
[Source](https://www.getty.edu/education/teachers/building_lessons/principles_design.pdf).
Supports balance, emphasis, eye movement, proportion, rhythm and unity/variety.
Not a mandate for symmetry, thirds, one composition or an objective beauty score.

## A07

**The Walt Disney Family Museum — Shape Language (2020 resource path).**
Museum education guide; read all five PDF pages, including exceptions on p.5.
[Source](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/2020-04/T%26T_ShapeLang_v9.pdf).
Supports shape as a communication tool and explicitly permits mixed / contrary
meanings. Does not support assigning moral character or demographic identity
from a triangle, circle or square.

## A08

**Stan Prokopenko / Proko — Mannequinization: Structure of the Human Body.**
Practitioner/instructor primary lesson notes, publicly accessible text only.
[Source](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/mannequinization-structure-of-the-human-body/).
Read What/Why Mannequinize and Basic Overview. Supports connected simple volumes
in perspective guided by gesture. Does not require one ideal body, a fixed
box/curve gender system, or access to the paid course.

## A09

**Animation Mentor / Anthony Wong — Building Appealing Character Poses (2026-04-07).**
Practitioner-led school tutorial; read article's workflow, four review elements,
negative space and weight discussion; did not watch the embedded video.
[Source](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-building-appealing-character-poses-for-animation/).
Supports judging meaning, silhouette, action flow and weight/counterpose from the
intended camera. Does not imply all poses must be exaggerated or asymmetric.

## A10

**Dana Boadway-Masson / Animation Mentor — Facial Animation 101 (2016-09-02).**
Practitioner primary tutorial; read public text on facial posing and eyes.
[Source](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/facial-animation-101/).
Supports face/body posing analogies and checking gaze in camera view.
Timing/lip-sync instruction is outside Artist's still-image scope. This is not
FACS certification or a scientific taxonomy of emotion.

## A11

**Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, with Deborah Nadoolman Landis —
Costume Design: Defining Character (©2014; file named 2015).**
Institutional teaching guide; read PDF pp.2–4, especially printed p.2's process.
[Source](https://www.oscars.org/sites/oscars/files/teachersguide-costumedesign-2015.pdf).
Supports script-led costume research and collaboration with director,
cinematographer and production design. Does not certify historical authenticity,
garment construction or permissions for the included film imagery.

## A12

**Krita Manual 5.3 — Painting with Assistants.** Official tool manual.
[Source](https://docs.krita.org/en/user_manual/painting_with_assistants.html).
Read perspective, two-point, orthographic and vanishing-point logic sections.
Supports guides, distinct parallel-line families and scale-preserving reference
projection. A tool guide cannot validate a painting's spatial design automatically.

## A13

**Walt Disney Animation Studios — Look Development.** Studio primary process.
[Source](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/look-development/).
Read Process, Deconstructing Materials and Iterations. Distinguishes surface
shape/relief, color and reflectivity work. Used for material analysis, not a
requirement to implement shaders, grooms or proprietary studio tools.

## A14

**Walt Disney Animation Studios — Lighting.** Studio primary process.
[Source](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/lighting/).
Read introduction and final color-key/collaboration explanation. Supports mood /
attention through color/light and working from visual-development color keys.
Film-specific looks are examples, not a palette to copy into The Stories.

## A15

**Walt Disney Animation Studios — Layout & Final Layout.** Studio primary process.
[Source](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/layout/).
Read Process and Layout Finaling. Supports interplay of camera, staging,
composition and neighboring-shot continuity. Local Artist/Cinematographer
boundaries are a project convention, not a claim that real studios separate them
in exactly that way.

## A16

**Krita Manual 5.3 — Color Managed Workflow.** Official tool manual.
[Source](https://docs.krita.org/en/general_concepts/colors/color_managed_workflow.html).
Read basic info, management, profiles and workflow guidance. Supports distinguishing
working, display and output interpretation, and consulting the print destination.
No single profile or view transform is prescribed for every project.

## A17

**W3C WAI — Images Tutorial (page updated 2026-04-08).** Standards body's guidance.
[Source](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/).
Read purpose categories and text-alternative guidance. Supports context-dependent
informative/decorative/functional/complex descriptions. Asset description alone
cannot prove the host's accessibility conformance.

## A18

**W3C WAI — Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1: Use of Color.**
[Source](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html).
Read criterion, intent and examples. Supports additional visual information when
hue carries meaning. Does not require monochrome artwork or prohibit color coding.

## A19

**Creative Commons — Attribution 4.0 International, Legal Code.** Primary license.
[Source](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en).
Read scope / other rights and attribution conditions (Sections 2–3). Supports
checking credit/notices/change markings and the limited scope of granted rights.
Not a blanket clearance of trademark, privacy or third-party rights.

## A20

**Creative Commons — Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International, Deed.**
Primary license summary; read terms and notices, not a legal opinion.
[Source](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/deed.en).
Supports recognizing the commercial-use restriction and checking actual terms.
The deed itself says it is a summary, not the legal license text.

## A21

**C2PA — Content Credentials Explainer, version 2.4.** Standards producer's explainer.
[Source](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/explainer/Explainer.html).
Read Goals/Non-goals and Core Concepts. Supports traceable, tamper-evident
provenance while distinguishing it from factual truth. Version pinned for this
research, not claimed to be the latest. A Markdown log is not a signed credential.

## A22

**W3C WAI — Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3: Contrast (Minimum).**
[Source](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).
Read criterion, text-size qualifications and incidental text exception. Supports
4.5:1 normal text and 3:1 large text where applicable. Not a ratio to enforce
between every painted surface.

## A23

**W3C WAI — Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11: Non-text Contrast.**
[Source](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).
Read criterion and graphical-object scope. Supports 3:1 for required graphical
information against adjacent colors, subject to its exceptions. Not a blanket
contrast certification for illustration or every page containing it.

## Retrieval exclusions

Blender latest and 4.5 manual pages returned fetch errors (402); Drawabox lesson
1/7 returned 403. Older graphics.pixar.com material/lighting PDF URLs redirected
to a general technology index. Search snippets for those PDFs were not used as
full-paper evidence. These are excluded from the accepted source set; use A12,
A13, A14 and A16 for the supported subset. No paid course/book was represented as
read. Recheck URLs/content before using this registry for materially changed tool,
license or standard behavior. Live page access verifies a route, not its permanence.
