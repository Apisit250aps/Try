# Shot lighting and color treatment

Use for practical or virtual lighting plans and low-light diagnosis. Claim IDs
resolve through [notes](../research/notes.md). Physics, craft and interpretation
are different kinds of evidence.

## Source before fixture

For each important light identify its narrative/world motivation, direction,
height, apparent size relative to subject, color, falloff/spill, and which shot
states it must support. A practical is a source visible in the scene; it may need
offscreen augmentation. Motivated light feels related to a plausible source;
an intentionally expressive scheme may depart from that plausibility if the
approved style supports it. Do not invent a visible lamp when canon says the
room has none. [P10/P05: C; H/S01]

Quality describes shadow-edge hardness, not brightness. Increasing apparent
source size softens shadows; bounce/diffusion can enlarge the effective source
but spill needs control. Key/fill/separation/background describe functions;
they are not a compulsory four-fixture diagram. [P10: P/C]

| Need / when | How to test (H) | Trade-off / counterexample / repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shape face or object | Put the key where it reveals required features through highlight/shadow; test both entry and exit poses. | A pleasing cheek light can hide the eye that carries the beat. Change height/angle or add restrained bounce. |
| Gentle quality with directional shape | Use a relatively large source to one side; manage fill and spill. | Soft light can still have high contrast. If background washes out, flag/grid the spill rather than making the source harsh by default. |
| Hard patterned light | Use a smaller apparent source/defined occluder and track shadow direction. | Hard does not mean evil or suspense. It may fit clear daylight/comedy. If pattern masks the clue, reposition it. |
| Contrast / low key | Control shadow-side fill and background exposure, while retaining the essential read. State the measurement convention if giving a ratio. | Low key is not simply global underexposure. If the face and set merge, add separation or alter blocking/background value. |
| Practicals in frame | Balance visible bulb/shade against useful face light; augment from a compatible direction if needed. | A bulb can clip long before the face reads. Dim/control it or use another motivation; don't assume a LUT rescues it. |
| Light through movement | Plot key zones and transitions; test contact, sit/stand and reverse views. | A face entering shadow can be intended, but a key jumping sides across adjacent shots needs a reason or a documented cheat. |

## Low-light worked decision

Brief: a character reads a worn token beside an existing desk lamp at night;
look bible asks for textured shadows, not a black silhouette. Proposed treatment:
keep lamp world-left; use a broad offscreen bounce from that direction to lift
face/token, limited fill from the room, and a darker background with one readable
window edge. Keep the token's recognition mark above the local shadow texture.
Test with a held pose, then the actual hand path; monitor acquisition clipping
and noise under the selected transform. [H, based on P10/P11]

Why not “raise ISO and make it moody”? Gain cannot repair missing directional
structure, a clipped lamp or a focus miss. A proposed f/2 opening may help exposure
but lose the token as it moves. Alternative: f/4 with more motivated illumination;
costs output/control, gains depth tolerance. If power or generation budget is
tight, hold camera and prop on one readable plane rather than add an orbit and
a focus pull. These are proposals; no lux/exposure/identity result is measured.

## Color, continuity and look ownership

Separate source color, surface color, WB/tint and display/grade interpretation.
A WB value names a balance, not the emotional color of every surface; mixed
sources may retain different casts. Check costumes, skin and clue readability
together. Temperature alone does not specify a source's full spectrum. Consult
the camera/source documentation for technical matching. [P10/P11: P]

Yale's color discussion explicitly rejects exclusive color meanings. Record
“amber recalls this story's kitchen” as a contextual hypothesis or established
motif, never a universal fact about warmth/family or a culture. Check whether
the brief supports that association. Do not use hue alone to distinguish an
essential prop if shape/value can preserve readability. [P05: I; H]

Artist's look bible owns the recurring palette, material response, stylization
and global color/light rules. Cinematographer records scene/shot deviations,
motivation, source positions, continuity state and approval needs. Disney's
lighting account illustrates working from visual-development keys; it does not
mandate Disney's looks for this project. [P15/S01]

For reverse coverage use world landmarks for sources, not “key camera-left” in
every shot. A motivated window may appear on opposite screen sides when the
camera changes. Check shadow direction, practical on/off, time/weather, exposure,
WB and look version. If a controlled cheat improves a face, document which shots,
the tolerated mismatch and an adjacent-shot test; don't silently relocate the
window or repaint Artist's bible. [H]

Reference images are inspection/analysis inputs, not automatic permission to
redistribute frames. Use original examples and keep provenance for supplied
assets; source access does not grant reuse rights. [S01]
