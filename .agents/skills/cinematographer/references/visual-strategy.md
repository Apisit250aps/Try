# Visual strategy and editorial purpose

Read this for intent, shot function, composition, formats and sequence rhythm.
Claim IDs link to [notes](../research/notes.md). The decision exercises below are
local craft heuristics (H), not measured psychological effects.

## From script to a visual hypothesis

Write a short chain: **script evidence → audience task → visual choice → cost →
test**. Track character knowledge separately from audience knowledge. Do not
invent a new clue to make an attractive shot work. [P01/P02/P04]

Example: the script says the courier notices the broken seal only after sitting.
Frame the intact-looking envelope during entry; after the sit, rotate its existing
broken edge toward camera. An insert now confirms it. Cost: a prop/action match.
Test: no earlier angle, reflection or focus pull exposes the edge. Counterexample:
if the script requires the audience to know first, withholding it removes the
dramatic irony; show the edge before the courier's look instead.

For an open strategy brief, compare meaningful approaches: shared wide frame
preserves relational performance; isolated singles control attention but can
conceal physical proximity. Choose according to the scene, rather than shooting
every size automatically. Convert an abstract note like “estranged” into a
proposal such as “both remain visible, separated by the empty chair established
in the script.” Director owns whether that staging fits performance. [H]

## Shot size and function are different decisions

Scale labels are relative to subject and surrounding shots. Specify what must be
visible, including hands/props, rather than relying on CU/MS alone. Establishing
is a spatial function; a master is continuous coverage of the scene's main action.
A shot can do both, one or neither. Coverage is the set of usable alternatives
and cut connections, not just the number of angles. [P01/P03; H applications]

| Choice | Use / execution | Cost, counterexample, diagnosis and remedy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establishing / re-establishing | Show the relationships needed for the next action: door, table, exit. | A generic exterior may establish city but no usable room geography. If the chase route is unclear, show relevant landmarks together. |
| Master | Carry the playable action through its turn, preserving physical relationships. | Expensive to reset; can disclose a hidden object too early. Choose a restricted master or acknowledge no all-seeing master is intended. |
| Single / close view | Isolate a choice, detail or change that matters now. | Too tight may omit the gesture that explains the face. Widen or cover hand and face together before adding another insert. |
| Two shot | Observe simultaneous behavior and relationship. | One face may occlude the other. Stagger depth or alter blocking, with performance approval. A sustained two shot may outperform mechanical shot/reverse-shot. |
| OTS | Retain a partner's presence and relative position while privileging one face. | Shoulder can consume a vertical frame or obscure a prop. Use a thinner shoulder edge or clean single; preserve eyeline. |
| POV | Show a particular observer's optical access. Pair with a motivated look when needed. | A free-floating “POV” at the wrong height implies false knowledge. Match position, visibility and look target. Subjective experience can also use nonliteral views. |
| Reaction | Hold the listener's observable response to a specified stimulus. | Premature reaction may imply foreknowledge. Mark trigger and performance room; do not cut only because the speaker changes. |
| Insert | Make a detail of the ongoing action readable at the necessary moment. | Can telegraph importance or break hand state. Match orientation/ownership and preserve the entry/exit pose. |
| Cutaway | Offer relevant context outside the currently framed action. | An arbitrary cutaway cannot repair contradictory geography or missing cause. Use a motivated view with a defined temporal relation. |

## Compose an unfolding frame

Treat composition as allocation of information, not a geometry contest. Brown's
preview discusses hierarchy, balance, depth and frame edges; these support a
choice, not a compulsory thirds grid. [P04]

| Problem / when | Decision experiment (H) | Trade-off / observable repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focal hierarchy competes | Name first, second and deliberately hidden reads; compare a value thumbnail and delivery-size frame. | Blur is one tool. If the key is lost, reduce competing brightness/motion, enlarge its silhouette or give it a clean background. Do not blur a simultaneous threat the audience needs. |
| Depth is ambiguous | Separate foreground/middle/background by overlap, scale, light or parallax; keep a landmark. | Flat staging can be intentional in graphic animation/comedy. Add depth only when it clarifies the relationship. |
| Negative space feels empty | Assign it a job: an expected entrance, isolation, caption region or breathing room. | If nothing depends on it, reframe. If it anticipates an entrance, do not fill it with incidental motion. |
| Balance is static | Test centered vs offset placement across the turn, using movement or another object as counterweight. | Symmetry can sustain discomfort or comedy; asymmetry is not automatically dynamic. Record the scene-specific interpretation. |
| Framing cuts action | Track hands, gaze, headroom and prop path across the whole move, not only frame one. | An intentional crop may conceal information. If it hides the essential release/catch, widen or change action staging rather than label it cinematic. |

Angles are viewpoints with informational consequences. A high view can clarify a
route; a low view can hide what lies beyond a table, or simply share a child's
height. Neither fixes status or morality. Cultural associations, genre learning
and the surrounding shots affect interpretation; describe it as a hypothesis.
Likewise colors do not have exclusive meanings. [P01/P05]

## Aspect ratio and 9:16 readability

Design for the actual gate/delivery crop. Cropping a wide two shot can remove its
relationship or clue. For vertical scenes test depth-stacked/staggered blocking,
sequential reframing or singles before squeezing two tiny faces side by side.
Re-check eyelines, scale and hands in each version. [P01; H]

Record output dimensions, ratio and intended display size. Obtain the actual
platform/UI overlay template when available; there is no universal 9:16 safe
rectangle. If unknown, declare a provisional normalized action-safe rectangle
and caption reservation, then test with the real overlay before delivery. Keep
essential action/eyes/props clear of it; allow decorative overflow. A portrait
virtual sensor differs from cropping a landscape gate: record which. [S02]

Example local review target: 1080×1920 output, preview at 270×480, provisional
action-safe x=.10–.85, y=.10–.78, caption band y=.80–.92. These values are a
test setup, not a platform requirement. If the token cannot be identified there,
enlarge its silhouette/contrast or change the shot. Pixel count alone cannot
prove recognition. Never declare this target met without inspecting frames. [H/S02]

## Information, motifs and emotional contrast

For each disclosure record what is visible, occluded, out of focus or offscreen
and who can perceive it. Use the approved story order; query an ambiguous reveal
before changing its consequences. A motif repeats a meaningful relationship
(e.g., a doorway initially divides two people, later frames them together),
not merely a lens or color used everywhere. Track setup, recurrence, variation
and payoff by beat IDs. [P02/P04; H]

ASC's accessible Morano preview describes withholding visibility as a strategy
for one film. That practitioner account supports considering absence, not a
general claim that hiding more always increases engagement. [P22]

Hitchcock's public account describes using hand/eye/object views and reactions to
build a situation in *Sabotage*. Treat it as one practitioner's method, not proof
that long takes cannot involve viewers. The local alternative is to stage the
hand and face legibly in one shot; test both in an animatic. [P02]

Contrast can carry change without escalation: a fast sequence may settle into a
quiet wide; a cheerful setting may hold an uncomfortable exchange. Ask what
actually changes in performance/information. Avoid making every painful moment
dark, handheld and tight. [H]

## Sequence, duration and medium

Separate proposed narrative shot order, optional coverage and production setup
order. Every essential beat needs a readable path through the proposed cut;
every optional angle needs a reason or can be removed. Give Editor the entry,
trigger, important hold and exit for each shot plus handles/alternatives, rather
than dictating an exact cut on every line. Duration is an estimate until read,
blocked, animated or cut; overlapping audio/action is not counted twice. [P03/P06]

| Context | Strategy to test (H) | Failure / alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suspense / mystery | Establish enough threat or uncertainty to support the approved audience question; control occlusion. | Hiding everything creates confusion. Reveal geography while withholding identity if the script permits. |
| Fast action | Show goal, route, contact and consequence; vary density around decisive actions. | Short shots alone do not explain cause. Add a clear approach/contact view or simplify motion. |
| Calm emotional scene | Let attention/performance change within a stable frame, with adequate reaction room. | A long hold with no new perception can go inert; shorten or adjust staging with Director. |
| Comedy | Make setup and payoff readable in relation; consider holding both participants together. | Cutting to the gag too early spoils surprise; too late hides it. Test timing alternatives with Editor. |
| Documentary / naturalism | Plan available viewpoints and light for events that may not repeat. | Do not fabricate reenactment as observation. Prioritize usable evidence over completing an ideal coverage grid. |
| Graphic / stylized animation | Use deliberate flatness, silhouettes, held poses or nonphysical light consistent with the project. | Photorealistic DOF/motion blur may damage a pixel or paper look. Agree with Artist on rendering constraints. |
| Vertical short | Make the required inference readable quickly at use size, with room for interface/captions. | More cuts or larger faces alone cannot replace spatial context; build a vertical master or clear landmark. |
