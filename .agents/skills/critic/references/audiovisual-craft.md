# Audiovisual craft review

Review only modalities actually available. Vocabulary supports diagnosis; it
does not prove taste or audience response. [P05–P07/P13](evidence-map.md).

## Art, design and cinematography

Inspect the intended viewing size/crop when available: focal hierarchy,
silhouette, figure/ground, shape/material language, readable gesture, expression,
prop/clue visibility, palette and repeated style. Compare with the actual art
bible/model sheet, including version and approval source. Describe the visible
region before interpreting it; do not treat all stylized anatomy as a defect.

Identity comparisons need matched conditions where possible: character's left
vs screen-left, view orientation, mirror/reflection, pose, occlusion, costume,
light/color transform, injury/age/scene state and authorized redesign. A white
mark vanishing in an unobstructed same-side view is a stronger drift candidate
than a hidden mark in reverse view. A changed silhouette may be an expressive
pose; check the project's permitted deformation range before assigning severity.

For a shot ask **what must be perceived, felt or withheld, and how does the shot
serve that?** Examine framing, scale, foreground/background, eye-line, blocking,
movement/occlusion, depth/focus and lighting motivation/contrast. Do not infer
an exact focal length, exposure or light ratio from appearance alone. Separate
Artist's designed look from shot execution and Cinematographer's plan; a dark
frame can support concealment yet hide a required clue. Explain that trade-off.

## Continuity, coverage and editing

Compare action/time/space and screen relations around actual cut boundaries:
entry/exit, prop ownership/contact, gesture, eye-line, axis, screen direction,
light state, knowledge/reveal and intentional transition. Establish the axis
from provided geography; two reversed stills alone may not prove a violation.
Check camera travel, re-establishment, neutral views, insert/ellipsis and designed
disorientation. Deliberate crossing may work; stated intent alone does not prove
its execution is clear. Report “orientation risk” separately from “wrong rule.”

Coverage is **sufficient connections for the required inference/performance**,
not a mandatory count of wide/medium/close-ups. Identify the missing connection
and feasible assembly alternatives; an intentional unbroken shot may need no
traditional coverage. Plans describe possibilities, only footage can establish
whether a usable reaction or insert actually exists.

Inspect whole rhythm plus local edit relation: action anticipation/completion,
eye-trace, emotional reaction, speech/silence, montage/parallel action and release.
A long shot is not inherently slow; many cuts do not inherently raise engagement.
Murch's priorities are a practitioner lens, never validated scoring percentages.
Consider cause/information before prescribing duration changes. Record measured
playback intervals separately from estimated script/board allocations.

## Performance and sound

Performance: compare readable intention, listening/reaction, timing, gesture,
pose/line of action, facial/vocal choices and relation to others. Naturalism is
one convention; deadpan, broad comedy, ritual, stylized animation and melodrama
can be intentional. Do not infer a performer's psychology or talent from a
fragment. Separate script, direction, take selection, rig and animation causes.

Sound: when actually audible, inspect speech and story-essential cues under the
intended playback conditions, masking, sync, transitions, spatial/source relation,
music perspective/counterpoint and silence. Intelligibility may matter for one
clue but deliberately be limited elsewhere. Cite words/events/time ranges that
can or cannot be made out; don't invent measured peaks/LUFS/clipping from a
transcript or perceived harshness. Sampled playback cannot certify the mix.

No audio supplied → mix, vocal performance and intelligibility **not-assessed**;
planned cues can still be reviewed in text. A verified intentionally soundless
work → speech intelligibility N/A, but visual accessibility may still apply.
“Silent animation” can mean dialogue-free or fully soundless: follow the actual
brief and label an assumption if unresolved. Never add VO to repair a no-VO lock.

## AI-generated artifacts and technical defects

Inspect actual images/frames/audio, including joins and interactions: character
or object drift, duplicated/missing limbs or props, hand/contact errors, unstable
text/symbols, occlusion/geometry changes, flicker/texture crawl, impossible
unintended motion, unstable shadows/reflections, lip-sync or sonic artifacts.
VBench motivates separate dimensions; no score is claimed without running its
methods, and no benchmark proves current model limitations or narrative quality.

Distinguish a visual symptom from a cause: a prop popping may originate in an
asset, reference, generation, comp or edit; route a diagnostic question instead
of assigning blame without evidence. Deliberate metamorphosis, smear frames,
stutter, collage and impossible physics may be part of the style. Check reference
locks, scene function and temporal context. Still images cannot establish motion
smoothness or absence of flicker.

Technical defect: a reproducible mismatch with an applicable delivery requirement
or an unintended artifact supported by evidence. Creative defect: a reasoned
mismatch with intent. The same blur might be a required dream effect, a lost clue
or a technical focus failure. Identify which claim the evidence supports.
Use the actual current house/platform standard for codec, dimensions, fps,
safe areas, color/loudness or caption format only if relevant; fetch official
docs/version then. No fixed platform numbers are baked into this skill.
