# Judgment, access and uncertainty

Support and limits: [P02/P08–P13](evidence-map.md). Use the relevant concerns,
without converting every creative sketch into a compliance audit.

## Audience, emotion and interpretation

Separate **intent** (brief), **observable cue**, **reviewer's response**, and
**audience-effect hypothesis**. “The pause may invite uncertainty because the
hand remains suspended” is supportable; “this increases retention by20%” requires
actual appropriately designed data. Audience segments are not monoliths. Do not
project a model's preference, one culture's symbol reading or a review panel's
agreement onto all viewers. Engagement, comprehension, emotion and completion
are distinct constructs; a benchmark proxy cannot establish all of them.

A useful proposed test names the target viewers, actual revision, viewing context,
neutral question and decision it informs. For example, after a first unprompted
view ask what caused the door to open, then compare a minimally revised cue.
Record sample/recruitment, uncertainty and competing explanations if performed.
Do not ask leading questions that reveal the intended solution, or claim a
small informal test proves general retention. Strategist owns audience research.

Allow multiple readings; evaluate each against cues. A nostalgic scene may feel
unsettling to another viewer. That may be valuable or conflict with a tightly
specified brief. Explain the tension and let Director/user choose; do not force
agreement through repeated rewrites. An intentionally broken rule can still
miss its intended effect, but diagnosis requires actual evidence of that gap.

## Accessibility and technical requirements

Assess applicable access needs from the medium/use: essential sound information
in captions, essential visual information in description/descriptive transcript,
reading time/placement, color-only clue dependence, legibility at output scale,
flashing/motion concerns and player access where relevant. A silent film is not
automatically accessible to blind viewers; captions alone describe no visual plot.
Preserve a soundless main-work lock while proposing a separate access track,
version or descriptive transcript with an owner and verification need.

Do not infer WCAG conformance, flash safety, loudness, codec, color or platform
compliance without the applicable current standard, actual deliverable and
necessary tests. Fetch official docs only when those details affect the task;
record version, method, tool and measured result if tested. Flag visual flashing
for a qualified check without inventing a seizure-safety threshold or forcing
repeated exposure. Unknown delivery context stays an explicit limit.

## Factuality, representation, originality and rights

- Separate an in-world claim, a character's belief, fiction/alternate history,
  and an external factual assertion. Locate it; check relevant current primary
  sources for consequential real-world claims. Do not “correct” fictional rules
  using real physics unless realism is the chosen contract. Medical/legal or
  other high-stakes claims require qualified/current review, not Critic certainty.
- Discuss who is framed with agency, whose perspective is missing and what
  stereotypes/power relations a particular choice may reproduce. Cite the scene
  and acknowledge cultural expertise and interpretive limits; avoid inferring
  the maker's intent, identity or prejudice. Suggest an appropriate contextual
  reader when consequential; do not invent community endorsement.
- Originality can involve a distinctive relation, execution or perspective on
  familiar material. Compare **specific expression** only when both actual
  sources are available. Shared tropes/style/plot resemblance do not establish
  plagiarism or infringement. Missing comparison evidence means not-assessed,
  not an accusation. Attribution, plagiarism and copyright are different questions.
- Record asset/reference origin, permission/license evidence and scope, credits,
  transformations, consent/likeness/voice concerns, AI involvement when known
  and unresolved rights. Accessible online is not necessarily reusable. A license
  screenshot or credential is evidence to verify, not an automatic clearance.
- Provenance assertions may be verified without being true or sufficient for
  copyright/consent. Absent metadata does not prove human origin or illicit use;
  an apparent defect does not prove AI origin. No detector or similarity score
  alone is a verdict. If needed, preserve the actual locator and route to the
  user/producer and qualified reviewer for the applicable jurisdiction/use.

Report concrete concerns with confidence and evidence sought. Do not label all
unknown rights a production blocker by default; tie a `needs-evidence` gate to
the actual delivery requirement/risk. Creative approval and legal permission
remain separate. No tool upload, contacting people or publication is authorized
merely by this review workflow.

## Model and review limitations

Disclose unavailable audiovisual perception, sampled frames, partial transcript,
missing language/cultural expertise and any unverified technical measurement.
Do not describe a text reading as “watched/heard.” Surface internal contradictions
in feedback and give a disconfirming interpretation before finalizing major notes.
Separate confidence in an observation from uncertainty in cause or impact.

LLM judge studies motivate checks for position, verbosity, prestige and self-
preference, but do not prove this Critic is unbiased after following a checklist.
Keep author/reviewer identity and prior involvement visible. If shown earlier
notes, document that the reading was informed. Maintain unprompted/held-out
examples for later evaluation; repeated improvements on known tests can overfit.
Do not reward a revision simply because it follows your proposed wording.
