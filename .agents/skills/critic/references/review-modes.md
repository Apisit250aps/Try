# Review modes and coverage

Select the requested stage and actual evidence. Partial reviews remain useful
when their bounds are explicit. [P01/P16](evidence-map.md).

| Mode | Focus and useful output | Evidence limit / appropriate check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept | Premise clarity, organizing interest, tonal/genre promise, audience assumptions, distinctive choices and feasible constraints | Compare possible interpretations; a concept cannot prove scene rhythm, performances or eventual engagement. No compulsory three-act outline. |
| Story bible | World rules/chronology, character goals/agency/arc, themes, knowledge/disclosure, open promises, episode state, authoritative vs proposed facts | Cite fact IDs/versions and consequences. Missing adjacent episodes mean scoped canon review only. |
| Screenplay | Scene purpose, causal/informational links, playable action, dialogue/voice/subtext/exposition, intended emotional turns, essential sound/image clues, estimated duration and production demands | Read text, not imagined footage. A scene can sustain mood or observation rather than turn. A read-through/animatic is a next test, not an already measured result. |
| Art / character assets | Hierarchy, silhouette/readability at intended scale, style/material/shape language, expression/pose, identity locks and scene-state consistency | Compare actual inspected views/regions with the approved reference. Account for perspective, pose, mirrored view, lighting, costume and authorized redesign. A prompt-only review evaluates specification. |
| Shot plan / storyboard | Required inference and shot function, staging/blocking, visual attention, light/reveal intent, screen direction/axis/eyeline, coverage and state transitions | Boards can demonstrate some composition; shot text describes a plan. Neither proves temporal motion, intelligibility or final coverage execution. |
| Animatic | Beat readability, planned transitions, shot durations, performance staging and causal/emotional rhythm | Inspect played timing if possible; rough holds, scratch audio and absent polish are stage constraints. Flag only where they block the current decision. |
| Rough cut | Whole sequence effect, causal order, performance selection, coverage, rhythm, sound/image relation, missing placeholders and technical failures | Distinguish temporary from intended; test root cause before prescribing trims. Audio temp status does not make all intelligibility questions irrelevant. |
| Final cut | Creative intent plus agreed delivery/access requirements, actual continuity, sync/audio, generated defects, credits/provenance uncertainties | Final-stage review requires the actual deliverable and relevant platform/house specification. Unknown technical tests stay not-assessed; no blanket certification. |
| Cross-artifact | Brief → canon → script → assets → shots → cut; ID/version, clue order, costume/prop state, knowledge/reveal and authorized deviations | Report exact pair of conflicting revisions. Do not treat an old plan as superior to an authorized cut revision. Mark propagation work and owners. |
| Standalone work | Infer modest provisional intent from supplied artifact, describe its patterns and offer alternate readings | Avoid demanding nonexistent production records; no invented canon or approval history. Explain how a different brief could change evaluation. |

## Change the questions with the form

- **Short-form:** inspect entry, essential inference, processing/reading time,
  payoff or deliberate openness under the actual slot. No universal “hook in
  three seconds,” shot-frequency or retention threshold. Preserve meaningful
  silence/holds. 30 seconds in a beat sheet is an allocation until timed.
- **Animation:** compare staged pose, anticipation/contact, performance and
  identity across views/frames. Judge squash/stretch, held frames, impossible
  physics and deformation against the style; do not demand live-action realism.
  An animatic is not a final animation test.
- **Episodic/serial:** compare inherited states, character knowledge, recurring
  rules, reset/serialization design, episode-level progress and open promises.
  A local unresolved question may be a season promise. Name unseen episodes;
  do not certify the season from one recap.
- **Long-form:** track setups/payoffs, changing relations, recurring motifs,
  thematic tensions, sequence rhythm and whole-work consequences. Sampling
  scenes cannot certify the entire arc; no required page-number formula.
- **Experimental/non-narrative:** identify another organizing principle (rhythm,
  association, duration, sensory pattern, argument); character arc and causal
  resolution may be N/A. Explain why a criterion applies before lowering it.

## Inspection manifest

For each input record `supplied → accessible → inspected` independently. List
actual ranges, modality, tool/method and limitations. If only frames3 and19 were
seen, report the still comparison; flicker, motion, edit rhythm and sync remain
not-assessed. If a video player lacks audio capability, say visual-only; if no
visual tools exist, review text/metadata only. Screenshots of waveforms do not
establish speech intelligibility. No universal sampling interval makes a full
inspection; include action boundaries/problem regions and disclose omissions.

For an overall recommendation, state how much of the **required** scope was
assessed. “No issue found in this excerpt” is not “no issue in the film.”
Do not infer missing final deliverables are defects at a concept-stage review.
