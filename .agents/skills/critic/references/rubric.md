# Rubric, severity and quality gates

These anchors and thresholds are **local design decisions**, informed by
[P09/P10](evidence-map.md), not empirically validated measures of artistic worth.
Prefer an argued review without a total when numbers add no useful decision.

## Severity is consequence; confidence is support

| Severity | Anchor | Example / counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| blocker | Prevents the explicitly requested next decision/delivery because a mandatory requirement fails or a critical dependency cannot be established | The sole final file cannot be opened; a locked no-identifiable-person delivery includes a confirmed prohibited face. A personal dislike of the ending is not a blocker. |
| major | Substantially undermines a core intended inference, arc, identity, continuity or experience; requires meaningful revision | A key transfer needed for the resolution is absent from the inspected complete script. One unresolved mystery clue in an excerpt is not automatically major. |
| minor | Located, limited cost; core inference remains usable | A nonessential prop's unexplained state jump in otherwise clear continuity. Severity can rise if the prop is the decisive clue. |
| suggestion | Optional improvement, alternative interpretation or preference with a stated benefit/cost | A different palette or longer reaction might suit another reading; retain the current choice if its purpose is preferred. |

For missing critical evidence report **review blocked / needs-evidence**, not
“the film is defective.” Separate a mandatory gate's `unknown` from `fail`.
Creative severity scales with the brief and stage, not how confidently the note
is phrased. Prioritize consequence/dependency before cost of repair; list cost
separately. Do not dilute a major concern into politeness or inflate a suggestion
to win an argument.

An optional enhancement without an established mismatch is a **suggestion**,
even if it concerns a small visual detail. Do not call it a minor defect merely
because it is small. State the actual target: inability to read an unrequested
action (for example writing, when only holding a notebook is required) does not
establish failure of the supplied pose brief.

Confidence: **high** = direct sufficient evidence with alternatives checked;
**medium** = evidence exists but context/cause or interpretation remains uncertain;
**low** = weak/partial evidence, plausible risk requiring another check. State
what would change the assessment. Confidence is not a numeric probability.
A high-confidence observation may support only a medium-confidence impact or
low-confidence cause; split those when material.

## Agree the rubric before scoring

Record brief revision, required scope, dimensions, weights, anchors and chosen
gates. For a small task use provisional priorities and say so; no permission
round is needed just to offer critique. Ask when a missing priority changes a
consequential recommendation. Do not choose new weights after seeing a result
to get the preferred winner. If criteria change by an owner decision, version
them and reassess comparisons consistently.

| Dimension | What evidence can establish | Typical stage relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent / tone / genre | Located cues support or conflict with the declared experience; alternative readings noted | All |
| Narrative / character / theme | Causal/information relations, agency/arc/steady-state, recurring patterns | Concept through cut, where relevant |
| Scene / dialogue / exposition | Purpose, playable tactics, voice/subtext and disclosure | Script/performance/cut; may be N/A for abstract work |
| Visual / identity / shot function | Readability/style locks, framing, blocking/light, continuity and coverage | Art/boards/footage at appropriate scope |
| Performance / edit / sound | Actual readable reactions, rhythm, assembly, audible cues/intelligibility | Actual performed/timed/audio evidence; plans get separate plan criteria |
| Access / factual / provenance / delivery | Specific applicable requirement and evidence for its status | Use-dependent, with specialist limits |

Each selected dimension gets its own observable target and counterexample. The
shared ordinal anchors are:

- **0:** demonstrated failure of the named core target.
- **1:** partially supports it; material unresolved conflict in inspected scope.
- **2:** supports it with bounded limitations or a minor weakness.
- **3:** consistently supports it across the required inspected scope, with
  specific evidence and relevant exceptions/counterevidence considered.
- **N/A:** outside the agreed criterion/brief, with reason.
- **not-assessed:** applies but evidence/capability/inspection is insufficient.

Anchor examples: for “key transfer readable before S030,” 0 = no transfer in
complete available sequence;1 = written transfer but simultaneous obstruction
leaves the causal inference unclear;2 = transfer specified/visible with a minor
distraction;3 = required sequence inspected with clear cause and preservation
through revisions. A script may score a **script-realization** criterion; it
cannot score3 for the **film's actual readability** without viewing the film.
For “soundless visual story,” do not punish absence of dialogue; choose visual
inference and pose/action targets, with dialogue N/A.

If a weighted summary is requested, use `sum(weight * score) / sum(weight)` over
assessed applicable dimensions, and show the 0–3 scale, weights and denominator.
Show N/A exclusions and **assessed weight / all applicable weight** coverage
separately. Required not-assessed scope prevents a whole-work verdict even when
the partial average is high. No unsupported decimals or percentages presented
as scientific precision; report the ordinal profile first. Totals are navigation,
not approval. Unequal intervals in an ordinal scale limit arithmetic meaning.

## Mandatory gates separate from scores

Use only gates applicable to the task; do not invent a legal/delivery approval
flow for a concept sketch. Gate states: `pass`, `fail`, `unknown`, `N/A`, with
evidence/authority and scope.

1. **Review integrity:** all substantive observations are tied to actual evidence
   at the claimed modality/version; no fabricated inspection, timing or results.
2. **Authority:** current user intent/locks respected, disagreements exposed;
   Critic has not silently revised or claimed Director approval.
3. **Required scope/inputs:** critical applicable evidence exists and was
   inspected; otherwise `unknown`, recommendation bounded or insufficient-evidence.
4. **Brief/delivery gates:** specifically agreed non-negotiables such as a slot,
   essential clue, safety/access delivery or cleared-use requirement. Identify
   the owning standard/decision and appropriate verifier; no generic certification.

A gate failure or an open blocker stays prominent despite high creative scores.
An unknown gate requires evidence, not an invented failure or automatic pass.
“Ready-for-owner-decision within assessed scope” may carry minor/disputed notes;
it never means approved for release. If an owner accepts creative risk, retain
the Critic recommendation and the distinct decision record.

## Calibration and disagreement

Use a small common set of actual examples with the same brief, locators and
rubric version. Reviewers rate independently first, record evidence, then compare
dimension/severity differences. Resolve factual disagreements by inspecting the
artifact; clarify anchors when criteria were misunderstood. Preserve credible
interpretive disagreement, initial ratings and adjudicated ratings separately.
Consensus is not proof of validity, and a majority does not decide artistic truth.

If multiple reviewers are unavailable, offer an explicitly same-reviewer second
reading or alternative lens; do not call it independent. When available and
authorized, a fresh reviewer can receive brief/artifact/rubric without the first
verdict. For alternatives, mask maker/model labels and vary presentation order
where practical; report order effects rather than pick the convenient verdict.

For an actual reliability study record paired units, sample size, missingness,
initial rating matrix, agreement/disagreement and design-appropriate statistic.
Exact agreement is matching eligible pairs / eligible pairs; it is not a quality
score. Ordinal weighted kappa or other methods need their assumptions, declared
weights and uncertainty examined. Do not import healthcare thresholds, compute
reliability from post-discussion consensus or call one pilot representative.
