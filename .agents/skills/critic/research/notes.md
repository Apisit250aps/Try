# Research synthesis and decisions

Read alongside the [registry](sources.md) and
[claim map](../references/evidence-map.md). This is synthesis of inspected
portions, not a literature-completeness or professional-expertise claim.
E/F/H/I/T/D labels remain distinct throughout the skill.

## From analysis to useful criticism

S01's university analysis guide and S07's critical essay distinguish evidential
analysis/discussable criteria from liking. S06 offers a broader contextual role
for criticism. **D synthesis:** combine close reading with a purpose-relative
recommendation, retain strengths, and label preference. **Use:** all review modes.
**Counterexample:** a production checklist alone misses meaningful ambiguity;
a purely appreciative essay may not give a maker a next action. **Trade-off:**
an issue register improves follow-through but can flatten an open interpretation;
preserve the critical argument and competing readings above the register.

## Information, dramatic craft and artistic exceptions

S08 supplies distinctions for information access; S09 offers practitioner
attention to exposition/subtext. **H/D synthesis:** compare world truth, character
belief and disclosure before diagnosing a hole. Evaluate explicit dialogue by
what it does in the relation, not a ban on telling. **Use:** script/bible and
episode reviews. **Counterexample:** an observational scene or intentionally
unreliable telling need not satisfy a conventional causal arc. **Trade-off:**
too much ambiguity can hide a necessary immediate inference; too much repair
can erase mystery. **Test:** C01/C03/C10.

## Formal craft without rule policing

Yale's S02–S05 show craft vocabulary and alternatives. S10's excerpt prioritizes
qualities of a cut while explicitly qualifying numerical priorities. S11 tests
event segmentation under a particular film/task. **H/D synthesis:** inspect
function before identifying a spatial mismatch as an error. **Use:** shot/cut,
performance, design and sound reviews. **Counterexample:** montage or stylized
acting may intentionally disrupt realism. **Trade-off:** respecting intent is
not immunity from examining execution. **Test:** C04/C05/C07/C16. No source
establishes our severity levels or guarantees comprehension.

## Audience response is a separate empirical question

S22 investigates transportation/belief/evaluation; S11 event segmentation.
These are different constructs and do not yield retention predictions.
**D synthesis:** every engagement claim needs a stated basis: reviewer response,
craft hypothesis or actual study. **Use:** intent/tone/pacing review. **Test:**
C02/C06/C07. **Trade-off:** a useful hypothesis can guide an inexpensive first-
view comprehension check without being advertised as population evidence.
Unprompted viewers, varied context and cultural expertise remain untested here.

## Calibration, independence and disagreement

S15 describes norming with located examples; S16 discusses rater consistency
and its limitations. S12/S13 illustrate candor and hierarchy effects in animation
production. S18 studies biases in model judging of chat responses. **D synthesis:**
fix criterion definitions before comparison, preserve independent first ratings,
inspect disagreements, separate factual correction from valid taste differences,
and use a fresh reader when available. **Counterexample:** consensus after a
leader speaks can look reliable while concealing dissent; a consistent model
can consistently use the wrong criterion. **Trade-off:** calibration costs time
and can encourage conformity, so use exemplars plus counterexamples and held-out
cases, not repeated reward for a single house style. **Test:** C11–C13/C15.
Our 0–3 anchors, weights, gates and cycle default are D, not S15/S16 measurements.

## Media capability and responsible claims

S17's author project separates generated-video defect dimensions. S19 explains
distinct accessibility components; S20 separates provenance from truth; S21
distinguishes ideas and expression in U.S. information. S23 frames contextual
visual literacy. **H/D synthesis:** inspect the actual modalities and artifact,
check applicable requirements, avoid unsupported origin/plagiarism claims, and
escalate concrete uncertainty with evidence sought. **Counterexamples:** a
prompt cannot prove generated identity; no audio does not prove a bad mix;
missing metadata does not prove misconduct. **Trade-off:** excessive caution can
hide an actionable concern, so identify what is known and who can resolve the
specific remaining question. **Test:** C08/C09/C14/C16. No legal/compliance
or forensic certification is claimed.

## The Stories ownership and integration

**D01/D02 only:** Critic owns independent evaluation and verification; Director
owns creative decision, makers own revisions. Neighbor contracts distinguish IDs,
versions, draft/reviewed/approved and plans vs produced evidence. Critic preserves
those distinctions through [local adapters](../references/production-contract.md).
S14's dailies context motivates stage-aware review but does not define this
project's authority. **Trade-off:** isolated semantics avoid concurrent edits but
cannot prove a future Director consumer will accept the packet. **Test:** C15
plus the separate actual-neighbor exercise in the evaluation record.

## What would change the skill

Use observed failures, an actual contradictory contract, new production standards
or more representative review evidence. Add a narrow correction tied to that
evidence. Do not extend the skill by copying whole manuals or converting each
test result into a universal rule. Broader audiovisual/audience and multi-rater
validation remain in the [gap map](audit.md).
