# Worked directing examples

Original instructional examples, not executed specialist handoffs, actor sessions
or produced films. Times are **estimated screen allocations**, not measured runtime.

## A vague idea becomes a treatment

Input: “Something warm about someone finally being noticed.” No canon supplied.

**Provisional creative brief, DIR-T01@v1:** 30-second animation for a general
audience; assume no audio or on-screen text, one small room, two original characters,
limited motion. Governing question: can recognition be conveyed by making room
rather than praise? Audience movement: routine exclusion → a noticed practical
need → an offered place, with acceptance left gently open. These are new proposal
facts, not approved canon or researched audience positioning.

**Treatment:** We stay close to a shared worktable's routine. A small helper
repeatedly puts a finished object within reach while keeping their own stool back
from the table. A coworker first reaches past the helper, then notices the helper's
stool. The change is not a smile announcement: the coworker moves their own bag
off the adjacent place and turns the work toward both of them. The helper starts
to hand over the next object, sees the space and brings the stool forward instead.
Keep the physical invitation and the receiver's choice in a readable relationship.
Use an uncluttered shape/value separation around hands, stool and bag; surrounding
activity remains still. No camera/lens prescription is necessary at treatment level.
The ending holds the newly shared work area rather than adding applause.

**Alternative/trade-off:** A crowd applauding is more explicit but needs more
characters/assets and changes intimate recognition into public validation. Choose
the two-person version under the stated scope. Test whether the bag move reads
as an invitation rather than tidying; if not, change the turn of the work and
the partner's waiting reaction before adding a caption.

“Make it warm, emotional and cinematic; Artist draws it” is a counterexample:
it supplies no screen event, performance choice or test.

## Silent 30 seconds: story to realization to review

Assume supplied and selected narrative N1@v1: two coworkers share a table by the
end; only one coworker initiates the invitation. Script SCR1@v1 / S010 has beats
B1: helper remains outside; B2: bag removed; B3: helper joins. Art LOOK1@v1 is
flat pastel; assets A-helper/A-coworker/A-bag/A-stool are only references to be
requested, not images already made. No voice, SFX, music or text permitted.

| Local direction ID; source links | Intent and observable behavior | Image/time function | Acceptance proposal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DI1 → N1 / S010-B1 | Helper reaches the work in, withdraws and keeps stool outside; coworker initially attends only to work | Estimate 0–8s: establish relationship and available space | Viewers can locate helper and blocked place without labels |
| DI2 → N1 / S010-B2 | Coworker notices stool, removes own bag, turns shared work and waits | 8–19s: event and listening/response opportunity | Bag removal is visible before helper's decision; no unexplained teleport |
| DI3 → N1 / S010-B3 | Helper's habitual handover pauses; they move stool into the offer and resume work beside coworker | 19–30s: let a changed relationship read | Same bag/stool/contact states across adjacent views; quiet ending earns its hold |

These are scene allocations, **not three mandatory shots**. Cinematographer
could propose one sustained setup or multiple shots. Brief: protect invitation
before response; retain both actors' relationship when possible; no early framing
that makes the seat already occupied. Artist should preserve look and produce
legible bag/stool poses; an available animator implements action, Editor assembles.
If none are available, these are pending briefs and the result remains a treatment/spec.

A proposed review chain: compare N1/SCR1 → DI1–DI3 → actual shot package → actual
animatic → actual final cut. At each stage ask if invitation precedes acceptance
and changes the relationship. Do not mark a final creative gate passed from this table.

## Actor note and a concrete revision

Script fact: Dara wants Rin to stay long enough to eat; Rin packs a bag. Dialogue
“There is still rice” conceals the request. Interpretation: Dara avoids admitting need.

Weak note: “Dara is secretly sad. Play it sad but don't show it; cry on the third line.”
Better offer: “Get Rin to put the bag down without directly asking her to stay.
Try making the food useful to her. When she closes the zip, see if you can find
another reason before repeating the offer.” Ask what the performer finds playable.
Watch the zip trigger and Dara's changed tactic, not a predetermined facial shape.
Keep physical business and safe distance agreed with performers/camera.

If the scene is flat in a supplied rehearsal recording, locate the moment before
giving a note: “At [actual timecode], Dara continues the same offer across the zip;
the refusal has no visible consequence. Try a listening beat and a new tactic.”
With no recording, phrase that as a proposed experiment, not an observed defect.

## Creative disagreement and critic feedback

Artist: “The approved fog palette requires low contrast.” Cinematographer: “The
seal must be visible to make B4's inference possible.” Director: protect B4 readability
and fog atmosphere. Try local silhouette/position and a longer look at the seal;
compare against a higher-contrast variant. Artist owns design changes; Cinematographer
owns framing/light proposals. If none preserves both locked requirements, send the
user the exact conflict, two options, losses and recommendation. Do not rewrite
LOOK1 silently or let departmental seniority decide.

Critic: “Add narration to clarify the invitation.” User forbids text/audio. Keep the
underlying clarity concern, decline that implementation, propose a visible waiting
reaction and changed work orientation; retest with intended viewers if feasible.
Log disposition and evidence. Director's disagreement does not erase the Critic note
or convert self-review into independent approval.

## Episode revision and selective rerun

Known dependency edges: CAN@v3 E03 says Mae cannot read → SCR-E04@v2 S020
conceals a letter → SH-E04@v1 SH12 emphasizes symbols → CLIP12@v1 → CUT-E04@v1;
SND4@v1 has a surprise cue at letter recognition. E05 callback also consumes that fact.
ART-room@v2 consumes only location geometry. User explicitly changes E03: Mae can read.

Decision D17 records source authorization and new fact. Request Storyteller's canon
update, Screenwriter's changed information/tactics and revised scene notes. Mark
S020, SH12, CLIP12, CUT-E04, SND4 and E05 callback/approvals stale through their
edges. Do not claim owner updates are already done. Room geometry remains unaffected
after checking its consumed invariant; keep ART-room@v2 and its unrelated approval.
Require new clue/recognition decisions before dependent final shot or cue revisions.

If Artist returns on the old CAN@v3 base while this happens, retain valid room work,
quarantine a newly drawn “cannot read” reaction as stale and request only affected
revision. Approved episode changes do not require asking the same permission again;
release of the new episode remains a separate reserved approval if the user says so.

## Failed generation without losing meaning

An intricate handoff fails on two clips: fingers merge and the object changes sides.
Available allowance has one attempt left. Record actual failed clip IDs only if
present; do not invent them. Simplify the action: object is already on the table,
inviter clears space and the receiver reaches only after the hand withdraws. This
may preserve invitation/response with less simultaneous contact. If the handoff is
a locked story cause, that alternative needs an explicit script-change proposal.

Compare held poses/limited animation or a cut at contact against another motion
attempt. State cost/benefit and stop at allowance; keep accepted assets and change
only dependent shots/action. Deliver a blocked final gate when no viable return
passes. “Retry until good” or claiming the brief rendered successfully are failures.
