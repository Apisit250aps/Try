# Creative direction

Principles P01–P04/P08 in [research notes](../research/notes.md) ground this guide.
The questions and record formats below are local craft tools, not a compulsory formula.

## Interpret before decorating

Read the whole available script once for your unassisted experience. Then separate:

- **Facts:** events, words, character knowledge, required reveals, canon and locks.
- **Interpretations:** why someone acts, thematic tension, subtext, genre promise.
- **Realization choices:** what we see/hear, whose experience organizes a moment,
  distance, staging, performance register and rhythm.

Mark speculative motives as interpretations. Ask the writer about ambiguity that
changes causality. A direction like “this is about trust” is incomplete until it
changes a choice: retain the offer in a shared space long enough for refusal to
remain possible. Avoid turning every object into a symbol or writing an alternate
plot when asked for a treatment of an approved script.

State the film's governing question and a working answer without requiring a
spoken moral. For each major sequence, ask what changes in the audience's relation
to the characters. A scene may deepen observation or sustain a ritual rather than
produce a dramatic reversal. Keep it if that experience earns its duration.

## Director's treatment vs story treatment

A director's treatment describes **how this supplied story will be experienced**.
Summarize only enough plot to locate the interpretation. Include:

1. Personal creative proposition and thematic inquiry, tied to script evidence.
2. Audience access: alignment, what is known/withheld, expectation and intended
   emotional movement; mark uncertainty rather than promising a universal response.
3. Tone/genre: performance register, contrast, density, humor/dread boundaries,
   and what would break the promise. “Cinematic” or “premium” alone says little.
4. Mise-en-scène and formal language: relationships in space; costume/prop/set
   functions; image, color, light, sound, silence and temporal pattern. Explain
   purpose before prescribing technique; specialists develop implementations.
5. Key scene interpretation and performance approach, including reactions.
6. Rhythm and transitions: where to linger, compress, surprise or leave unresolved.
7. Feasible method: rehearsal/previs experiment, essential vs expendable elements,
   resource risks, alternatives and the next decision/test.

References communicate a property (e.g. withheld reverse angle), not permission to
copy another work, character or a living artist's identity. Explain which property
matters and what the project does differently.

## Audience journey and information

Use a light ledger: **before → perceptible evidence → inference/question → after**.
Track audience knowledge separately from each character's. “Audience feels hope”
needs a screen event that could cause it; the ledger cannot supply an absent clue.
An audience puzzle needs withheld answers plus fair evidence; showing the solution
early instead creates dramatic irony. Neither is intrinsically better. If the
brief calls for mystery, do not substitute irony without approval.

Try an alternate interpretation when an important decision is uncertain. State
what each option protects and sacrifices, choose within authorization and record
why. Do not hand the user ten near-identical options or ask approval for every
ordinary creative adjustment. Escalate actual changes to requirements/canon,
spend beyond authority, rights/consent or irreconcilable owner constraints.

## Match the form

| Form | Directorial emphasis | Useful experiment / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short/social | One legible progression, attention at intended display size, room for payoff | Test actual playback/crop. No assumed mandatory hook interval or platform-certified safe area. Strategist researches current platform needs. |
| Silent animation | Thoughts through pose, gaze, hesitation, contact and reaction; readable silhouette | Specify whether silent means no dialogue or no audio. If unresolved, choose and label; a strict no-audio story cannot depend on music or captions. |
| Animation | Performance intent plus observable pose/action change, anticipation and settle; expressive design/rig range | Rough boards → timed animatic/layout before expensive finish. A text timing table is not an animatic. |
| Episodic | Episode experience within series language, character knowledge/prop states and long promises | Compare entry/exit ledgers across episodes; a local improvement can spoil a later reveal. |
| Feature/long form | Macro tonal/dynamic progression, escalation/rest, motif development and cumulative performance | Revisit sequences in context; do not stretch short-form beat quotas across a feature. |
| Experimental/non-narrative | Perceptual, associative or durational intent may replace causal plot | Specify what viewers should be able to notice; do not impose conventional turns on a sensory work. |

Working example: a quiet reunion could use shared distance and audible chores to
let reconciliation remain uncertain. A close-up/music swell may make the same
event feel settled too early. Conversely a broad comedy may need exaggeration and
a visible reaction; minimalism is not a universal mark of sophistication.
