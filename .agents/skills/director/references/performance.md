# Performance direction

P03/P04/P08/P11 in [research notes](../research/notes.md). These are adaptable
practitioner tools; neither realism nor an action-verb method fits every performer.

## Find something playable

For a scene, locate the circumstances, relationship, immediate want, obstacle,
stakes, what is said vs pursued, and the event that changes a tactic. Ask the actor
what they are trying before replacing it. Listen to the response and watch what
changes between partners; a technically correct delivery can fail to register
incoming information.

Translate an audience outcome into an offer to the actor:

| Result-only note | Playable alternative | Observe / possible counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Be sadder.” | “Keep her here long enough to finish the tea; when she reaches for the bag, offer one last practical reason to stay.” | Does the departure change the tactic? This presumes a supported objective; don't invent attachment that contradicts the script. |
| “Look guilty.” | “Get him to accept the receipt without checking the date.” | Can the partner's suspicion affect behavior? A guilt display would spoil a mystery if the character is innocent. |
| “More chemistry.” | “Win her attention while pretending you only need the charger.” | Does an offer meet a response? Don't mandate physical contact or boundary violations. |
| “Be more emotional on this line.” | “Make him give the key back. Try bargaining; after he pockets it, deny him the exit with the question.” | Is a new tactic motivated by an actual event? Physical blocking still needs agreement and safe staging. |

Give one useful adjustment at a time. A verb without a target/circumstance (“accuse!”)
can be as empty as an adjective. Alternatives include a concrete task, question,
fact, image, changed circumstance or letting the actor propose an experiment.
Keep interpretations compatible with canon. Do not mine personal trauma, humiliate,
surprise or deceive a performer to obtain “real” emotion. [P11]

Exact external form can matter for comedy, musical rhythm, choreography, stylized
animation or a technical match. Explain the purpose and agree on the physical
requirement; avoid turning line imitation into the default account of acting.
Voice direction should include addressee, intent, thought/listening turn and useful
timing context; recording/mix belongs to Sound Designer and actual performers.

## Rehearsal and staging

Choose rehearsal for its question, not as proof of seriousness:

- Read-through: listen for comprehension, relationship and rhythm before heavy
  correction. Table work separates text facts from possible motives/subtext.
- Relationship exploration or task-based improvisation: discover interaction;
  keep new history provisional and avoid silently importing it into canon.
- Blocking rehearsal: agree on paths, obstacles, prop contact, distance and turns;
  invite actor discoveries, then reconcile visibility, eyelines/light and coverage
  with Cinematographer and set/asset limits with Artist.
- Technical/previs rehearsal: test marks, reveal occlusion, complex contact or
  movement with placeholders. Use qualified people for hazardous physical work.

Lumet's thorough run-throughs and Payne's looser relationship preparation support
different needs. Resources, actor preference and material determine the mix; do
not require two weeks or a fixed take count. Rehearsing until a response becomes
mechanical is a signal to change the experiment or pause.

On production/review, identify the scene's position in the arc, the other person's
trigger and the take's continuity needs. Watch listening and aftermath as well as
speech. A reaction should answer an event, not merely decorate a cut. Keep notes
private when appropriate; communicate a single consolidated intention rather than
several departments issuing contradictory performance instructions.

## Animation and generative performance

Keep **motivation** separate from **motion realization**. An animator needs the
want, thought change and rhythm, plus a testable silhouette/action proposal; don't
reduce every thought to raised eyebrows. Example: hand reaches toward the last
chair, notices another person's bag, pauses, then moves its own bag onto the floor
to make space. Hold long enough for the choice to read, then test in animatic.

For a generative model, a playable human note is not necessarily an executable
prompt. Keep the creative note, then adapt it to supported observable actions,
subject/camera motion and reference states. Verify actual provider capability,
identity/contact continuity and the returned clip; a prompt is not a performance.
An Animator/performer may be an available capability without being a named installed
skill. Record the real implementer or leave implementation unassigned.
