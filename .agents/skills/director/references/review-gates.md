# Review, revision and acceptance

P07/P10–P14 in [notes](../research/notes.md). These gates are local decision tools.
Apply only the scope being delivered; don't delay an ordinary treatment behind
unrequested production approvals.

## Review evidence before scoring

Declare inputs and inspection: text read, images viewed, video played, audio heard,
timeline/timecode measured, receiver response or none. A thumbnail cannot establish
rhythm; a treatment cannot establish actor performance or audience response.
Distinguish observable artifact features from your interpretation of likely effect.

A useful note has **artifact/version + location → observation → intended-experience
consequence → proposed revision/alternative → owner → acceptance test → status**.
Example: “CUT@v2 at 00:12: seal is visible before the suspicion beat, so the viewer
may already know the answer. Editor/Cinematographer: try the approved occluded
angle until B04; compare inference before/after. Verify on the new assembly.”
This is a hypothetical example, not an assertion that a cut was inspected.

Prioritize: requirement/canon/consent contradiction; comprehension/causality;
performance/experience; continuity/feasibility; polish. Local severity depends on
intent: an intentionally fragmented film may welcome spatial uncertainty but still
must honor its requirements. Avoid sending a dozen unrelated adjustments at once.

## Acceptance scopes

| Gate | Evidence / acceptance question | Owner and approval meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief/treatment | Does the actual treatment make specific choices that satisfy the user brief and locks? Are audience hypotheses and trade-offs explicit? | Director self-review or creative approval within grant; user only for reserved decisions |
| Story/script alignment | Are causes, character knowledge and mandatory disclosures consistent across actual versions/IDs? | Storyteller/Screenwriter resolve their artifacts; unresolved contradiction blocks affected final realization |
| Rehearsal/previs/animatic | Does the inspected experiment show action, reaction, space and information at intended timing? | Implementer + Director creative review; absent experiment remains spec-only |
| Production returns | Do actual assets/takes/clips preserve required identity, contact, state and intent? | Specialist technical checks and Director creative check, scoped to inspected output |
| Assembly/final creative | Does the actual picture/sound experience sustain intent in context, including transitions and ending? | Director creative approval within authority; Editor implements final cut/export |
| Rights/safety/delivery/release, when applicable | Actual source/permission/consent and qualified safety reviews, delivery requirements, access needs, reserved release authorization | Relevant human/rightsholder/production approver; creative approval alone is insufficient |

No “approved” without source, scope and version. An approval for a treatment or
proof clip is not approval for all assets or publication. Respect existing user
authorization and avoid adding universal sign-off rituals.

For sensitive live work, agree boundaries before rehearsal/capture; involve qualified
intimacy/stunt/child-safety personnel as appropriate. Do not propose real dangerous
acts as an acting exercise. For AI assets/voices, keep provenance, consent and reuse
scope explicit; ask the responsible human/rightsholder when missing rights actually
block intended use. Do not certify legal clearance from aesthetic similarity or a
provider label. S13's consent practice and S14's contract scope are limited industry
references, not jurisdiction-wide legal advice or an AI skill's authority. Verify
current applicable terms with the responsible production when needed.

Consider captions, descriptions and intelligibility when relevant to the deliverable;
don't hide a vital story fact exclusively in inaccessible audio/color by accident.
If strict silence/no text is required, preserve that constraint and offer separate
access versions rather than adding forbidden narration to the master.

For skill maintenance, use the separate [textual pilot rubric](../tests/rubric.md).
It evaluates textual behavior, not produced-film quality.
