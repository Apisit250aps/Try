# Image, sound and time

P01/P05–P09 in [research notes](../research/notes.md). Director needs enough craft
literacy to make choices with specialists; their implementation remains theirs.

## Mise-en-scène and visual logic

Ask what relationships the space makes visible. Set geography, costume/material,
props, entrances, distance, gaze, movement and offscreen space can carry subtext.
Brief Artist with narrative function and approved look IDs; brief Cinematographer
with attention/reveal needs, performance paths and the intended proximity to a
character. Resolve the shared boundary: visual design/light response vs actual
shot lighting. Do not silently revise an art bible to accommodate a shot.

Check that the intended clue/gesture can actually be perceived at delivery size,
with the planned occlusion, contrast, duration and surrounding visual activity.
Beauty that hides a necessary action can break the scene. A technically imperfect
match can be acceptable if it preserves a stronger performance without confusing
space/time; record the deliberate exception rather than claiming perfect continuity.

Avoid symbol dictionaries such as “blue always means sad” or “wide lens always
means comedy.” Derive formal choices from this story and its established language.
Use a baseline and meaningful deviations. Lumet's described visual progression in
Network is a case of theme guiding a changing look, not a preset for other films.

## Coverage as options for experience

For each scene turn, ask which action, reaction, relationship or withheld view must
survive the edit. Cinematographer proposes shot/camera/light/coverage solutions;
Director chooses the experience, including when a shared frame is stronger than
separate coverage. Editor tests actual combinations and implements the timeline.

- Identify essential evidence, reaction and connection; optional coverage has a
  named use and cost. No mandatory shot count or “always shoot a master” rule.
- Protect eyelines, screen direction, action phase, prop contact/state, costume,
  light/time and character knowledge. Intentional crossings/ellipses can work if
  viewers can reorient; ask for a bridge only when needed.
- Compare a sustained setup (relational continuity, fewer joins, harder performance
  and staging) with cuts (attention control/options, more setup and matching cost).
- Consider handles and clean transitions with Editor; distinguish estimated screen
  time from generated/captured clip length and alternate takes.

## Sound is part of conception

Decide what is heard, by whom, from where and when. Sound can locate offscreen
action, restrict perspective, create expectation or contradict an image. Invite
Sound Designer early when this changes staging/time; don't reserve sound for
patching an unclear picture. [P06]

State narrative function, trigger, perspective and silence constraints; specialist
owns cues, assets, recording and mix. “Add sad music” gives little direction.
“Keep the departing footsteps audible after she leaves frame, until he commits to
following” offers a testable relationship between sound and decision. If the
project forbids audio, solve that relationship visually. Never claim temp music
is cleared or that muted playback tests an audio-dependent experience.

Jenkin's Bait account shows a deliberately different picture/post-dubbing method.
That does not negate early sound thinking; it demonstrates a specific formal
choice whose feasibility and performance costs must be understood. [P06/P07]

## Pacing and rhythm diagnosis

Separate event density from attention, performance rhythm, shot duration, cut
rhythm and sound phrasing. A shorter shot can feel slower if it repeats information;
a long hold can create tension or dead air. Identify the moment viewers have
received the information and what makes remaining time valuable.

Review in context and vary one decisive layer when possible:

| Symptom | Competing diagnoses | Small experiment / receiver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reveal feels flat | Already exposed; no reaction; insufficient setup; competing cue | Trace audience ledger, then test withholding/response hold; Storyteller/Screenwriter for story changes, Cinematographer/Editor for realization |
| Scene feels slow | Repeated evidence; unclear objective; no listening turn; redundant coverage | Cut one repetition or isolate the triggering response in a temporary assembly; Editor/Director, actors if performance cause |
| Sound seems late | Wrong sync; deliberate offset fails; action has no visible/audible cue | Inspect actual timecode/cue evidence; Sound Designer/Editor; no invented timing from a script |
| Look feels inconsistent | Unauthorized design change; world light change; grade mismatch; intentional progression | Compare bible, scene state and shot versions before asking for a global regrade |

Notes must identify an observation and hypothesis separately. With only a script,
offer a diagnostic test; don't assert that unseen footage, unheard sound or an
unmeasured cut has failed.
