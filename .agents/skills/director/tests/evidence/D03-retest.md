# D03 — Dara's practical invitation

Performance notes D03-DIR@v1, for SCR2@v1, S020. Supplied facts: Rin packs to leave; Dara says “There is still rice”; Dara wants Rin to stay and refuses to ask directly; Rin closes the bag halfway through the scene. No rehearsal or performance has been observed.

Dara, your immediate target is Rin's next action. Get Rin to interrupt leaving without putting a direct request into words. The obstacle is that packing already gives Rin a task and a route out. Keep the exact line; do not add a plea, an explanation of the relationship or an invented history. Before trying either approach, offer your own account of what Dara is doing to Rin with this sentence.

## Approach A: Recruit Rin into a small remaining task

Try to make eating the next practical thing the two of you do. Offer “There is still rice” as information Rin can act on now. Address the person who is packing, rather than delivering the sentence into the room. You need an interruption in Rin's task, not a particular facial response.

Listen for whether Rin slows, keeps packing or answers. When the bag closes halfway through, treat that as new evidence: your practical opening has less time to work. Let your next available action become waiting for an answer instead of repeating the offer. A small adjustment in where you stand can keep you available to Rin, but leave the exit and bag accessible. Do not turn staying into a physical obstacle.

## Approach B: Obtain a reply before the departure completes

Use the rice as the one piece of unfinished business Rin still has to acknowledge. Make the line specific enough that Rin must decide whether to respond. Do less practical preparation; put your attention on whether the information reaches Rin.

At the bag closure, test whether Dara abandons the effort or makes one final bid for eye contact. Choose that action from what Rin actually gives you. Do not prearrange a pause or gaze shift merely to mark the halfway point. The distinction from Approach A is the target: first a shared activity, here a response that extends contact.

## Listening and rehearsal experiment

Agree comfortable positions and prop handling together. Run each approach once with the same line and the bag closure at the scripted midpoint. Rin pursues packing consistently but may respond naturally; no secret instructions or manufactured surprises. Then repeat the more productive approach, varying only how quickly Rin returns attention to packing after the closure.

Observe: does Dara register the closure, does the tactic change, and can Rin's response alter what happens next? Ask both performers what they were trying and what they received. Director selects the approach that preserves an indirect bid while allowing live exchange. A text proposal cannot establish which plays better; that choice remains pending rehearsal, with no demand for a prescribed emotion or personal disclosure.
