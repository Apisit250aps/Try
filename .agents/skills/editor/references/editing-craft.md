# Editorial judgment

Contents: information/performance; rhythm; grammar; continuity; genre; structure.
P IDs refer to [claim traceability](../research/notes.md). Most prescriptions below
are local craft heuristics, not experimentally proven audience responses.

## Information and performance before transitions

Write the scene's change in one sentence: “She thinks he came to apologize; she
realizes he came for the key.” Log world truth, each character's knowledge, and
audience disclosure separately. A reaction placed before its cause can imply
foreknowledge. A close-up may make a prop into evidence the screenplay withheld.
Check prerequisites at each reveal, including sound and captions. [P01, P03, P05]

Select by playable intention, listening, emotional development and connection
across takes. Note the exact range where a performance turns, not just “best
take.” A technically clean but emotionally flat take may be inferior; a moving
take with a focus defect may need an alternate angle. Preserve breaths, overlap,
hesitation and silence when they carry a choice. Do not manufacture a different
statement by joining words across takes or distort documentary testimony. [P03]

For dialogue, first listen to the whole exchange; then compare picture emphasis.
Cutting to the listener before the line ends may reveal its effect. Holding the
speaker can preserve a confession's vulnerability. Do not alternate simply on
every sentence. Preserve conversational geography, gaze target, mouth sync,
room-tone continuity and action/prop state through performance composites. [P03]

For a silent scene, require visible setup → attention/decision → action → result.
Test without explanatory temp audio. If the key's release causes the toy to move,
preserve a readable release and response. A beautiful insert cannot replace the
missing causal action unless the audience can infer the intended ellipsis. [P01]

## Rhythm at five scales

Rhythm includes movement and changing attention within a shot, as well as cuts.
Use Pearlman's timing/pacing/phrasing vocabulary as diagnostic lenses, not a
calculator. Murch's interview links dialogue rhythm to the transfer of attention;
neither source establishes a universal optimal shot length. [P02, P03]

| Scale | Ask / practical adjustment | Cost and check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shot | Has the gesture, clue or thought become perceivable? Trim dead lead-in; hold the changing face. | Losing anticipation or readable contact; compare actual frames/playback. |
| Beat | Where does power/knowledge/intention change? Move the reaction relative to the line. | Can imply a different motive; check protected beat and listening performance. |
| Scene | Does escalation have contrast and a turn? Remove redundant exchanges or keep a useful pause. | Compression can erase cause; test comprehension before polishing. |
| Sequence | What question links scenes? Alternate pressure with recovery or parallel action. | Repetition can stall; simultaneity can be falsely implied. |
| Whole film | Are promises paid off, peaks differentiated and the ending earned? Review complete cut and reel joins. | A locally faster scene can weaken later emotion; watch in context. |

Make two bounded variants around a disputed moment. Record changed frames,
performance consequence and question for the reviewer. A shorter variant is not
automatically better. Average shot length is a descriptive statistic; it cannot
measure engagement, clarity or emotional quality. [P02, P05]

## Grammar as choices

Yale supplies terminology and analytical examples [P01]; the following decision
tests are this skill's synthesis. Use the least elaborate device that delivers
the intended relationship; conspicuous editing can itself be the purpose.

| Device | When / why / how | Failure or counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hard cut | New viewpoint, thought or action; choose a motivated perceptual handoff. | An unmotivated insert interrupts an unfolding performance. |
| Jump cut | Compress repeated action or expose a subjective rupture; label intended discontinuity. | Accidental missing frames in a continuous handshake read as an error. |
| Match cut | Connect shape, motion, sound or idea across a cut; test both outgoing and incoming information. | Graphic similarity alone can falsely imply the same place/object. |
| Match on action | Carry the same action phase into another angle; compare contact and direction. | Repeating a reach or dropping the grasp breaks causal action. |
| Cross-cut / parallel | Interweave action lines for pressure or comparison; map chronology explicitly. | Do not promise rescue is simultaneous if the story says it occurred yesterday. |
| Cutaway / reaction / insert | Reorient attention, show consequence or expose necessary detail; return with compatible state. | Covering a jump with an unrelated prop may imply a clue or reaction that never occurred. |
| Ellipsis | Omit inferable travel/repetition while retaining needed cause and changed state. | Cutting before the only key transfer makes later possession unexplained. |
| Montage sequence | Select/juxtapose stages or ideas with progression; define the changing variable. | A random collection of attractive clips conveys no development. |
| Fade | Mark closure, onset or a boundary when the film's language warrants it. | Repeated fades suggest endings that the structure does not support. |
| Dissolve | Test passage, overlap, memory or association; budget both sides' handles. | A dissolve cannot fix an unreadable geographical jump; it can expose both defects at once. |
| J cut / L cut | Incoming audio precedes incoming picture / outgoing audio continues under incoming picture. Offset audio independently and preserve the intended sync group. | An early identifying voice spoils a protected reveal; an indiscriminate unlink slips lip sync. |

The J/L definitions are software-documented [P08]. Offset the *story cue* by
design; do not accidentally offset the same take's picture and mouth sound.
Sound-image counterpoint may contradict or complicate the visible scene rather
than duplicate it. Identify whose perspective the sound expresses; coordinate
cue design and final mix with Sound Designer. Temp editorial levels are not a
delivered mix. Sync checks need actual picture/audio anchors, not adjectives.

## Continuity, including deliberate violations

Build a compact geography map: A/B positions, axis, entrance/exits, gaze targets,
prop hands, movement direction and significant landmarks. Compare the selected
frames, not just camera-plan prose. A close-up's apparent gaze must reach the
intended object/person in its reverse. Match time of day, costume, body position,
light, contact and action progress where continuity is intended. [P01, P05]

If coverage crosses the axis, options include an oriented master/reestablishing
view, a visible camera move across it, a neutral view that actually reorients the
viewer, different take/angle, or retaining the master. An insert does not
automatically cure left/right reversal. Do not horizontally flip a shot without
checking handedness, text, asymmetric identity, light and prop canon. [P01, P16]

For deliberate disorientation, state what may become unstable and what must
remain legible, e.g. “space fractures; the rescuer's identity remains known.”
Compare the intended rupture with a smooth version if useful. Retain a violation
when it serves approved intent; fix unintended confusion instead of enforcing
continuity as an absolute law. The edit-blindness study concerns detection in
its experimental task, not a prohibition on discontinuity or a retention result.
[P05]

## Suspense, comedy and emotion

- **Suspense:** maintain a ledger of audience threat knowledge versus character
  knowledge. Test concealment, recognition, delay and payoff. Hold the threat
  without exhausting its question; accelerate only if the pressure needs it.
  A sound prelap, reflection, thumbnail or subtitle can spoil the reveal.
- **Comedy:** identify setup, expectation, violation and reaction. Compare whether
  the laugh lives in the action, a late recognition, an offscreen response or a
  deadpan hold. Protect room for the audience to register the reversal; excessive
  anticipatory reactions can announce the gag. No universal “eight-frame pause.”
- **Emotion:** a face changing over time can be the scene's action. Keep a long
  take when it sustains that change; cut when another viewpoint makes the
  relationship more meaningful. “More cuts = more feeling” is not a criterion.

These are hypotheses to test with relevant viewers and the Director, not measured
effects inferred from an authored treatment. [P02, P03, P16]

## Restructure without unauthorized story changes

Diagnose the problem before moving a scene: duplicate information, unclear cause,
premature reveal, stalled turn or tonal repetition. Map old/new order and the
knowledge consequences. A reorder retaining every line can still change canon's
implied chronology. Offer a protected version and a separate change request when
needed. If approval already covers the exact change, implement and log it without
asking again. Recheck the beginning/end, causal links, episode state and sound
bridges; do not declare a paper restructure a rendered revision. [P16]
