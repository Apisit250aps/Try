# Dialogue, VO and action prose

Evidence classes and source limits: [P06–P08](../research/notes.md).

## Voice and intention — P07

Build voice from the person's attention, social position, relationship, vocabulary,
syntax, sentence length, preferred evasion and behavior under pressure. A verbal
tic is a surface cue, not an entire personality. Use supplied character facts;
avoid phonetic caricature and treating a culture/disability as shorthand for voice.

For a difficult exchange, privately write what each speaker wants the other to do
and the consequence of speaking plainly. Then choose the tactic: invite, flatter,
bargain, deflect, provoke, confess, refuse. Let the response change the next move.
Do not put this analytic scaffolding into every parenthetical.

**Subtext test:** what is said, what is meant, and what action/context lets a
viewer infer the gap? A line about a spare key can test whether someone may stay.
Without that relational context it is merely a line about a key. Indirection is
useful when candor has a cost. In a direct confession or a character who deliberately
refuses euphemism, clarity can be the most revealing choice. Dee Rees's distancing
exercise is one practitioner's method, not a requirement to back off three times.

**Voice diagnostic:** hide names and inspect whether viewpoint, syntax and tactics
still differ. This is a heuristic, not a blind recognition experiment. If characters
sound alike, change what each notices/wants or how they avoid loss; adding slang
to one speaker will not repair identical dramatic functions. For ensemble writing,
a listener's consequential action can do more than adding a token line.

## Rhythm, silence and exposition — P07/P08

Read aloud when possible. Test breath, stress, interruption, answer latency and
whether the final emphasis lands on the intended thought. A realistic transcript
usually contains material a scene does not need, but stylized, lyrical or overlapping
dialogue may suit the work. Do not apply a universal maximum line length.

Use interruption for an actual contest of attention or an urgent interruption,
not decoration. Distinguish a trailing thought from being cut off consistently
under the chosen house style. Parentheticals can disambiguate an addressee,
necessary language or a meaning-changing delivery; repeated emotional adverbs
usually ask the performer to compensate for unclear writing.

Silence needs behavior/context and a consequence: a held-out cup left unaccepted,
a delayed answer while a bus leaves, or an absent familiar greeting. “A long beat”
without a reason or observable shift can become dead time. Preserve processing
space after difficult information; do not fill every gap with a joke or VO.

Exposition works when somebody needs it, resists it, pays for it or uses it. If
both people know a fact, explain why they would mention it now: test a lie,
rehearse a cover story, correct a dangerous inference. A learner's question can
be honest exposition, but a new character who only asks for the audience's benefit
can drain agency. Test whether the facts change the following decision.

## VO is a choice, not a defect — P08

Use VO for subjective perspective, contradiction, compression, a distinct temporal
layer or material that cannot be enacted economically. Establish who speaks,
from when, to whom if relevant, and reliability. Compare three versions when
stuck: behavior alone, VO alone, behavior in counterpoint to VO. Select by the
intended experience, not a blanket “show, don't tell” rule.

If VO duplicates an obvious action, cut or change its function. Counterexample:
repetition can intentionally establish ritual, irony or unreliable self-presentation.
Avoid resolving every ambiguity through narration. Narration says what is heard;
audio direction, casting, processing and mix belong in Sound Designer's return.

## Action description — P06

Use clear present-tense observable actions, selected sensory evidence and legible
cause/effect. Name the actor, object and outcome where spatial ambiguity matters.
“Feels betrayed” can guide a reader briefly but cannot alone tell a production
what to show. Replace or anchor it with a choice: returns the unopened gift,
keeps the receipt, leaves the shared key. Avoid adding a melodramatic gesture
that changes character simply to make an emotion visible.

Write sufficient geography for understanding: before crossing a gap, establish
where the usable bridge is. Group prose by readable action units rather than
unbroken description; exact paragraph length depends on pace and complexity.
Important sounds can appear as story events without prescribing a whole sound
plan. A subjective line or poetic image can improve the read if a maker still
understands the event. Economical does not mean lifeless or forbidding all
interiority, camera language, adverbs or “we see.”

Specify an essential visual dependency (“The seal is already broken before she
touches it”), not an unsolicited lens, camera move or numbered coverage sequence.
If a precise shot is locked/supplied, preserve it and report conflicts. A needed
INSERT or reveal can be a script-level proposal, clearly distinguished from the
Cinematographer's approved plan. See [format](format-medium.md).
