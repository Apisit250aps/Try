# Revision, production and verification

Trace: [P10–P13](../research/notes.md). These are operational proposals and craft
heuristics; they do not certify a provider, production or accessibility standard.

## Revise by effect — P13

Turn a note into an observable target: “make it warmer” might mean a character
chooses to help before asked; “faster” might mean remove repeated information,
not remove the pause needed to understand a reveal. If a note's interpretation
would change locked meaning, state the conflict and offer alternatives. Don't
rewrite everything around a vague adjective without checking the implied outcome.

Work from function/causality → information and agency → scene tactics/turns →
voice/action → format/continuity/timing. This order is a default diagnosis, not a
required ceremony for a typo. Record the smallest change that produces the desired
effect. Re-read upstream setup and downstream payoff, not just the modified line.

A useful diagnostic pass asks:

- Can a performer/maker enact each consequential event? Does a character choose
  or respond in a way consistent with their known options?
- Does every pivotal inference have perceptible evidence available before use?
  Who can hear/see it? Does the solution require an unplanted rule?
  Verify the setup appears in the screenplay itself: a ledger or explanatory
  note cannot plant an unseen prop position or mechanical cause for the audience.
- Which lines repeat visible facts, flatten voice or explain the just-landed joke?
  Is a direct statement actually required here?
- What changes if a scene/line is cut? What necessary reaction or orientation is lost?
- Are elapsed time, travel, entry/exit, hand/prop state, damage, costume, weather,
  language and character knowledge consistent with previous and next scenes?
- Do the duration ranges and required holds fit the requested slot? What remains
  unmeasured? Does a note introduce a new resource/dependency?

Preserve earlier versions and scene IDs. Record changed/deleted/split/merged scenes,
notes resolved and new risks. Accept, modify or decline a note with rationale;
self-review is not independent Critic approval. For a review-only request, return
prioritized findings with scene/line locator, evidence, effect, targeted correction
and retest; do not silently apply them.

## Timing evidence — P11

Use beat windows for short work and scene ranges for longer drafts. Separate
**planned allocation**, **estimated plausible duration**, **observed read-through**
and **measured animatic/final duration**. Exact allocations summing to 30s do not
prove the animation can perform them. Include delivery-card and transition overhead.
For concurrent speech/action, estimate the longer critical path plus non-overlapping
holds; add sequential tasks. A read-through measures that performance, not the
final animation. Record method, speaker/tool, speed assumptions and uncertainty.

## Script breakdown and AI/animation constraints — P10/P12

For relevant scenes, extract cast/extra count, INT/EXT/day/night, location/set,
props and transfer/state, costume/injury, essential action, story-critical text,
interaction/continuity, sound/dialogue dependency, practical/VFX/animation needs,
and unresolved resources. State what appears on screen, not an invented asset
file. Distinguish required story function from suggested realization.

For constrained animation, separate action into understandable poses/events and
clear object ownership. Identify difficult multi-character contacts, occlusion,
small readable text, changing identity, crowds, transformations and simultaneous
precise gestures. These are **local risk hypotheses** to test in the chosen tool;
do not present them as measured limits of every AI model. No universal maximum
characters/seconds/shots is asserted. If there is a locked two-character budget,
offer a simpler staging/action solution before adding a third character.

Reuse locations/props when it preserves dramatic function; excessive reuse can
flatten contrast or make a world implausibly small. Propose simplifications with
their dramatic cost. A hard story dependency should not be silently deleted to fit
a generator. Do not generate images/video, provider prompts, music, coverage or
timeline assets unless separately requested and appropriate to Screenwriter scope.

## Accessibility and provenance — P14

Ask what information is lost without hearing, sight or color discrimination when
relevant to delivery. Identify essential speech/sound for captioning and essential
visual facts for description. Provide meaningful speaker IDs and a dialogue/text
transcript when requested. Plan space for description or an alternative described
version with the downstream makers; don't spoil a mystery before its intended
reveal in descriptive narration. A silent-film creative brief can retain a silent
primary version and plan a separate accessible version.

Avoid tiny text/color as the sole critical clue where a shape, action or duplicate
cue can carry the meaning. Do not insert VO into a no-VO script merely to claim
accessibility. Captions, translated subtitles and audio description serve different
needs. Actual timing, synchronization, player support and conformance require a
rendered delivery and testing; a screenplay cannot prove WCAG compliance.

External scripts in research are linked for bounded analysis, not copied into
output or relicensed by this package's MIT license. Write original examples.
For real work, record supplied source/rights status and unresolved permissions
without claiming legal clearance. WGA material is scoped to its covered agreements;
see S11 in the [registry](../research/sources.md). Do not treat an AI skill's role
name as a professional writing credit or claim training/adaptation rights from
public availability. Recheck current applicable requirements for real contracts.

## Receiver handoff check

Send artifact/version, canon version/status, stable scene mapping, must-preserve
information/reveal dependencies, visible/audio state, estimated durations and open
issues. Ask the receiver to restate any ambiguous requirement and return a specific
constraint. A real receiver check includes their response and a revised packet if
needed. Until then call it a **handoff prepared**, not accepted or production-tested.
