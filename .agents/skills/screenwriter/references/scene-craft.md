# Scene craft and audience experience

Decision principles are local syntheses with explicit evidence classes in
[P01–P06](../research/notes.md). Use them as diagnoses, not plot generators.

## Build a scene that does something — P01

Start with the scene's **audience task** (discover a cost, feel a separation,
recognize a routine), then the participant's immediate intention. An intention
becomes dramatic action when pursued through behavior: persuade, conceal, test,
repair, stall, protect. Obstacles may be another will, a physical limit, a social
risk or uncertainty; quiet observational scenes can work through contrast and
attention rather than confrontation.

Sketch only what helps: entry state → attempt → response → changed tactic →
turn → exit state/consequence. Distinguish the writer's purpose from the
character's knowledge; a character should not conveniently serve a reveal they
cannot know about. Stakes are what this specific person may lose or gain now;
raising global danger is not a substitute for intelligible personal stakes.

A beat ends when pressure, strategy, understanding or behavior changes, not every
sentence. A turn can be a refusal, permission, discovery, changed relationship,
physical reversal or a newly understood image. It need not be a loud surprise.
When revising, test alternatives: enter after greetings vs retain the greeting
because a ritual establishes who is excluded. Exit at decision vs stay for its
cost; cutting early can remove the emotional consequence the scene exists for.

**Diagnostic:** state the before/after difference and who causes it. If only the
location changes, consider deletion, combination or a more consequential choice.
**Counterexample:** a sustained vigil may intentionally resist change; test what
accumulating duration/contrast lets the audience perceive, not whether it has a
mandatory reversal. John August's omission test is useful pressure, not a ban on
atmosphere. Keep a scene whose removal damages tone, relationship or orientation,
even if a plot summary still makes sense.

For causality, connect scenes with “because of that choice/result…” rather than
only “and then…”. A useful repair for a passive lead is to give them a meaningful
choice among costly options. Do not rewrite their arc to make them conventionally
assertive: refusal, waiting, delegation and concealment can have consequences.

## Control information — P02

Track three columns at each reveal: audience knows; each relevant character
knows/believes; observable evidence supplied. A narrator's statement is not
necessarily objective truth. Distinguish a fact, an inference and a false belief.
For mystery, withhold the answer while making the needed evidence perceptible;
for suspense, reveal a threat and obstruct the response; for surprise, conceal an
expectation-changing detail but leave the prior action coherent on rereading.
These are different effects, not ranked levels of quality.

For an audience-facing mystery, check that the opening plants evidence without
already displaying the complete answer. Showing the culprit or full solution
first can create dramatic irony while leaving only the characters puzzled. Use
that approach deliberately when requested; otherwise preserve a real question
for the viewer. Clarity of setup does not require showing the entire explanation.

Dramatic irony needs an intelligible knowledge gap. If the audience knows a door
is locked but the rescuer doesn't, attempts acquire pressure. If the audience has
never seen the lock, “suspense” in the notes does not create it. Repair by placing
evidence where it can be perceived, or intentionally choose shared uncertainty.
Fair-play is a chosen mystery contract, not a law of every thriller or dream film.

Use a setup/payoff ledger for indispensable plants: first visibility, innocent
interpretation, later reinterpretation/use and the character who can act on it.
Do not add a last-second world rule or hidden specialist knowledge just to solve
a mystery. Ask Storyteller to resolve structural reveals; Screenwriter can alter
how an approved clue is presented within authorized scope. Do not hide from the
viewer an unmistakable fact a fixed POV plainly witnessed unless unreliability
is part of the agreed contract.

**Repair an exposition dump:** retain the facts needed for the next choice; give
someone a reason to request, deny, distort or test them; let evidence contradict
speech. Clear direct explanation is appropriate for consent, safety, procedure or
an intentionally frank character. Obscurity is not automatically subtext.

## Tension, pacing and transitions — P03

Tension can arise from incompatible wants, anticipated consequence, uncertainty,
social exposure or delayed recognition. Diagnose the missing mechanism: no
question, no cost, predictable outcome, repetitive attempt or unreadable geography.
Try one targeted repair, then mentally play the scene again. More interruption,
shorter paragraphs or higher stakes alone may create noise.

Pacing is the pattern of attention, density, anticipation and release. A quiet
pause after an irreversible choice can accelerate felt consequence; removing all
pauses can make a revelation impossible to process. Compare scene lengths and
functions across a sequence; do not set mandatory page positions or turn counts.
For features, inspect causal sequences/subplots and who carries each consequence.
Use an outline's structure as a hypothesis; escalate plot/arc redesign upstream.

A scene transition can connect cause/result, question/answer, contrast, repeated
action/image, an ongoing task or necessary sound. Write the relation the audience
needs; leave exact cut, dissolve, J-cut or shot matching to Editor/Cinematographer
unless an authorized script instruction is necessary to understand the event.
Ellipsis saves routine time but must not conceal a necessary cause. For parallel
action, distinguish simultaneous story time from merely adjacent presentation.

## Ensemble and multiple speakers — P04

Map each participant's want, private information, leverage and effect on someone
else's choice. Design shifting alliances, attention and reactions; equal line
counts are unnecessary. If removing a participant changes nothing, combine or
omit them unless their presence creates meaningful social pressure. A silent
witness can constrain what others dare say. Distinguish “quiet” from “absent.”

Give a reader a clear referent for every important gesture and pronoun. In a
crowded scene, re-anchor who moves/holds/sees the object before the turn. Track
who hears a disclosure: everyone in the room does not automatically hear a
whisper behind a closed door. If several plots intersect, causal contact is more
useful than alternating equal chunks of unrelated exposition.

## Comedy — P05

Choose the comic engine: a defended self-image, conflicting social rules,
misinterpretation, disproportionate tactic, physical process or status reversal.
Give the audience enough context to predict a pattern, then alter it intelligibly.
Escalation should change cost/strategy, not repeat a louder version of the line.
Rule-of-three and buttons can be options; deadpan, anti-climax and slow repetition
can work without them. Do not explain the joke immediately after its payoff.

Write a readable setup and playable event; place the decisive word/action where
its relation can register. A reaction or silence may be the payoff rather than a
line. Estimate a recognition/response allowance; do not certify “funny” from a
beat table. Table-read or animatic variants to test whether a pause supports
recognition or kills momentum. Preserve dignity and intended target when localizing;
pun equivalence, taboo, politeness and social status vary across audiences.

## Non-dialogue scenes — P06

Without speech, establish goal/attention through selective action, an obstacle
or contrast, then a perceptible change. One prop can carry intention, history and
choice if its state is legible. Do not replace missing dialogue with subtitles,
thought captions or an internal monologue unless the brief allows them.
“Silent” may mean no speech, no audible soundtrack, or just a scene with quiet
sound; extract the meaning from the brief and label any assumption.

A non-color clue, distinct silhouette or graspable physical relation may be more
robust than tiny screen text. Avoid turning every emotion into a generic sigh.
A repeated action with one changed detail can convey change economically. See
[worked examples](worked-examples.md) for contrastive repairs and the
[medium guide](format-medium.md) for short-form timing.
