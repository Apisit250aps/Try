# Original worked examples and counterexamples

All fragments here are newly written instructional examples, not adapted passages
from research scripts. Decisions use [P01–P14](../research/notes.md); none is a
claim that viewers have tested these scenes.

## Silent 30-second animation — P01/P06/P11

Brief: one robot, one room, no speech/VO/text, one flower; a failed repair becomes
care. Provisional standalone premise: the robot can move a pot but cannot revive
a snapped flower. “Silent” here means no audible soundtrack.

Weak: “The robot is sad. It learns that love is more important than perfection.”
This names the interpretation without an event a maker can perform.

```text
INT. WORKROOM - DAY

A snapped flower droops over its pot. A small ROBOT props the stem upright.
It folds at the break.

The robot supports the stem with both hands. The flower stands.
It lets go. The flower folds.

The robot reaches for the stem again. Stops.
It pulls the pot into the patch of sunlight and sits beside it,
cupping the drooping bloom in one open palm.
```

Planned allocations: establish/break 0–5s; first attempt 5–10s; supported test
10–18s; stop/choice 18–23s; new state/hold 23–30s. Hypothetical plausible range
26–36s; **unmeasured**. Test whether the support release, second failure and
final support read as distinct actions. If not, simplify the first attempt or
extend duration; do not silently revive the flower. Artist needs snapped/intact
state and hand contact; Cinematographer needs the support/release visible, not
a prescribed lens; Editor receives the final hold's purpose. This is a prepared
handoff only. The ambiguity “repair vs acceptance” needs a viewer/animatic check.

## Dialogue with subtext — P07/P08

Premise: sibling A wants B to stay, fears rejection; B wants an invitation.
Weak: “I am afraid you will leave, so I am indirectly inviting you to stay.”
That states both analysis and intention instead of enacting a social risk.

```text
INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT

NOK dries the same cup. MAI puts on her coat.

NOK
You still take two sugars?

MAI
Train's in ten.

Nok sets a second cup beside the kettle.

NOK
I boiled too much.

Mai removes one glove.

MAI
One, now.
```

The second cup and removed glove supply inference; the change is Mai accepting
a small invitation. Without the departure context the sugar exchange could be
ordinary hospitality. Counterexample: in a direct reconciliation, “Stay. Please.”
may carry greater risk and be the stronger line. Do not enforce evasion everywhere.

## Mystery reveal without a lecture — P02

Locked outline: a parcel was opened before delivery; the caretaker is innocent.
Setup: at arrival the parcel's seal has a crescent-shaped tear, already visible.
A second intact parcel establishes how a new seal looks. Later, an accusation
lands on the caretaker because she has scissors. Repair: the protagonist compares
the arrival photograph to the present tear, then lowers the scissors in her hand.
She returns the caretaker's keys instead of narrating every inference.

Track audience/protagonist/caretaker knowledge at each point. The photograph must
exist before the reveal and show enough to compare; inventing it only in the
solution is unfair under this brief. A testimony-based drama could choose less
certainty; fair-play is the contract here. If the photograph requires unreadably
small text, use the physical tear shape. Don't change the culprit upstream.

## Comedy timing and information — P05

A clerk guards an “unspillable” demonstration cup (described as a product claim,
not on-screen text). She catches a falling pen with extraordinary care. While
bowing to a customer, she tips the cup into her own pocket. She keeps smiling;
a single drip falls from her cuff. No line explaining the failure follows.

Setup: invested self-image and guarded cup. Reversal: successful minor rescue
causes the costly spill. Button: concealed consequence escapes. Try a clean event
vs an extra proud flourish; the flourish could sharpen status or telegraph the
spill. Allocate a recognition pause as an estimate, then time performance variants.
Physical legibility, not a mandated number of beats, decides whether it lands.

## Ensemble and transition — P03/P04

Three siblings divide a workshop. One bargains for the tools, one wants the room
empty, one silently packs their mother's unfinished chair. The silent sibling
forces the others to confront what “empty” means by carrying the chair to the
door and stopping. Give that choice a response rather than assigning everyone
an equal speech. Transition to an empty doorway in the next scene can carry
absence, but the removal of the chair must have a causal route. If the outline
locks the chair in place, propose a different action rather than breaking canon.

## Revision and episode continuity — P10/P12/P13

E01 ends with the only key snapped in a lock. E02 draft has a character casually
unlock the same door. Fix within canon: E02-S010 starts outside the still-jammed
door; the character chooses to negotiate through it. A note requesting entry
requires a plausible authorized route or a Storyteller decision; teleporting the
key intact is not a polish. Record the state before/after and affected later exit.
For localization, a joke relying on a character refusing an honorific needs a
replacement status action if the target language lacks that exact distinction;
word-for-word equivalence may erase the scene's conflict.
