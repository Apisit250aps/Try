# Research notes, synthesis and claim traceability

Read [registry](sources.md) for exact inspected sections, authors, dates and limits.
Each P ID below is linked from runtime guidance. Source-backed observations are
separated from local decisions. The detailed operational procedures, examples,
record fields and numeric evaluation thresholds are **local H/D synthesis** unless
explicitly identified as a convention. No empirical superiority is asserted.

## Cross-validation and disagreements

- **Format:** S01/S02 and S03 agree on recognizable elements but vary on Courier
  vs Courier New; S14 visibly uses shooting-scene numbering, unlike S01 submission
  advice. Conclusion: choose recipient/medium, don't enforce one universal style.
- **Scene purpose vs discovery:** S04 stresses writer-designed purpose; S06 warns
  against making a character merely deliver required information. Use purpose to
  diagnose/select, discovery to draft; then test both causality and credible behavior.
  These are compatible phases, not two authorities voting for a rigid rule.
- **Visible action vs VO/dialogue:** S03/S10 emphasize economical visual telling;
  S05 describes productive interior VO and S06 values dialogue. Choose the mode
  by its function, not a ban on telling. S13's opening provides an artifact example,
  not proof one method caused its success.
- **Economy vs rhythm:** S07's reduction exercise is personal; S14's pauses show a
  published counterexample to “remove every beat of silence.” Preserve meaningful
  reaction and social rhythm. A PDF cannot verify the performed timing or laughter.
- **Production:** S12 links writing to resource feasibility; S18 confirms the
  action/detail and revision role in animation. Neither tested AI providers.
  AI-specific risk flags remain local proposals.
- **Mystery/tension:** S16 supplies a direct craft account of curiosity vs confusion
  and anticipated events; S09 provides a comedy example of knowledge asymmetry.
  The knowledge ledger is a local implementation, not their prescribed schema.
- **Adaptation/serial:** S19 supports selection/invention; S17 contrasts repeatable
  TV situations with feature closure. Apply these as contextual questions, not
  “all TV resets” or “every film must resolve everything.” S18's broad industry
  writer role is intentionally narrower in The Stories' ownership split.

## P01 Scene purpose and playable change

Evidence **E**: S04 asks what a scene needs to accomplish, who belongs and what
omission would lose. **H synthesis**: combine audience task with character intention,
attempt/response and a consequence; use before/after states as a diagnostic. Do
not require conflict or a turn in every scene. A sustained ritual may earn its
place through perception or emotional accumulation. Supports scene-craft and the
packet's objective/turn fields. Test: S01/S02/S05/S06 scenarios in [tests](../tests/scenarios.md).

## P02 Information is staged, not just withheld

Evidence **E**: S09 discusses audience/performer knowledge in a comedy setup; S16
distinguishes curiosity from confusion and known/unknown suspense. **H synthesis**: track
facts/beliefs/evidence for each observer; use the same ledger to diagnose irony,
suspense, surprise and a chosen fair-play mystery contract. Fair-play requirement
comes from a brief, not a universal source rule. Supports scene-craft and mystery
example. Test: S03/S06 scenarios; comprehension testing remains unperformed.

## P03 Pacing and scene relations

Evidence **E**: S04 considers duration, alternate entries and preceding scenes; S16
considers anticipation and causal delay; S17 discusses format-dependent breaks. **H extension**: compare causal sequence, density, release and
processing time; transitions can carry cause, contrast or a question. No mandatory
page positions. Long-form structural reliability remains untested. Supports
scene-craft/format-medium; test S04/S08.

## P04 Agency and ensemble

Evidence **E**: S04 tests necessary participants. **H extension**: map wants,
leverage, witnessing and consequential reactions; preserve quiet participants whose
presence changes others' options. Equal line counts do not establish agency.
Supports scene-craft; test S02/S08.

## P05 Comedy needs readable relations

Evidence **E/O**: S09 links planted ordinary details and perspective to a joke;
S14 scenes 101–102 contain pauses and conversational status play. **H synthesis**:
write setup, intelligible reversal and response allowance; test alternate timing,
not a mandatory three-beat pattern. Actual humor requires audience/performance
feedback. Supports scene-craft/worked-examples; test S04.

## P06 Audiovisual writing and silence

Evidence **C/E/O**: S03 treats screenwriting as audiovisual and present-tense;
S10 recommends considering alternatives to dialogue; S12 preface discusses turning
thought into production; S13 opening shows behavior before explanation. **H**:
anchor interpretation in performable evidence; diagnostic prose may include a
brief reader cue, but supply an event. A strict camera/interiority ban is unsupported.
Supports dialogue-action and silent example; test S01/S05.

## P07 Voice, tactics and subtext

Evidence **E**: S05 ties avoidance to social consequences; S06 favors discovering
character speech; S07 works on spoken rhythm; S08 discusses thought anchors.
**H synthesis**: test intention/risk, what is said/meant/inferred, differentiation
without names, listener action and read-aloud rhythm. Do not treat an unperformed
name-hiding exercise as a blind experiment. Supports dialogue-action; test S02/S08.

## P08 Exposition and VO

Evidence **E**: S05 distinguishes inner VO from duplicative exposition; S06 warns
against predetermined information delivery. **H synthesis**: compare behavior,
narration and counterpoint; provide facts that support an immediate choice. Direct
explanation can be the right dramatic act. Supports dialogue-action; test S02/S03.

## P09 Format is contextual

Evidence **C/O**: S01/S02/S03 define conventional script elements; S13/S14 demonstrate
real variations. **D**: preserve the recipient's current house style, keep internal
IDs outside submission pages, report plain text separately from paginated output.
A formatting guide's recommendations are not universal law. Supports format-medium;
test S08; no actual PDF/FDX pagination check yet.

## P10 Local contracts

Evidence **D**: user's explicit ownership specification plus inspected Artist
production-contract and repository roadmap at baseline d13dc0f. The cross-role table,
state/version fields, stable script IDs, proposal/escalation path and receiver
acknowledgment are The Stories interface proposals. **No external source is claimed

## P11 Duration and compression

Evidence **E**: S04 asks how much screen time to spend; S10 questions overloaded
short scripts. **H/D**: beat-level critical-path timing, uncertainty ranges and
separate allocation/estimate/measurement. Example seconds, speaking rates and
test tolerances are local planning choices, not published laws. This is deliberately
not a claim that one page always equals a minute. Supports format-medium and
revision-production; test S01/S04/S07.

## P12 Adaptation, episodic continuity and feasibility

Evidence **E/G**: S12 supports production constraints; S18 animation action detail;
S19 adaptation by selection/invention; S17 repeatable TV situations vs feature
closure. **H/D extensions**: function-equivalence map, inherited state ledger,
plot-change escalation, localization checks and AI risk flags. S12's later chapters
were not read. Neither a universal AI limitation nor cultural/series-room
expertise follows from these sources. Supports format-medium and
revision-production; test S05/S06/S08. Localization and provider-specific behavior
remain priority research-depth gaps.

## P13 Revision by observable effect

Evidence **E/G**: S10 supports notes/redrafting; S07 a personal reduction exercise;
S18 responsiveness to notes and S19 iterative drafts/editor collaboration. **H/D**: translate a note to
an acceptance target, preserve IDs/versions, inspect setups/payoffs and downstream
state. Change-history schema is local, not an industry standard. Supports
revision-production and template; test S07.

## P14 Accessibility, rights and AI provenance

Evidence **G**: S15 plans description against essential visual information, available
space and delivery method. S11's inspected opening scopes AI guidance to covered
work; it cannot establish this project's rights. **D**: plan caption/description
handoff without claiming compliance, keep a no-VO primary script when locked, and
mark source/credit uncertainties. Public scripts are bounded research, not training
or adaptation clearance. Supports revision-production; test S01/S05/S08.

## Case-study observations, not copied examples

**S13, opening printed pp. 1–2:** specific physical behavior and an offered meal
carry interpersonal distance before a backstory explanation. The entry also uses
precise time labels and selected camera language. Inference: action and sparse
speech can support interpretation together. This is an observation of an entry
draft; the whole screenplay and film were not assessed.

**S14, scenes 101–102, printed pp. 1–4:** an ordinary activity, pauses, unequal
exchanges and a correction of terminology establish relational texture. Inference:
low-key comedy need not resemble constant punchlines. Shooting numbers and revision
marks demonstrate document purpose, not instructions for all spec scripts. Its
cover's copying restriction is retained as a research limit; no excerpt is packaged.
