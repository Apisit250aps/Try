# AI audio, provenance, consent and accessibility

Read P07–P09/P11–P13 in [notes](../research/notes.md) for source scope. The record
fields and stop conditions below are local production safeguards. Provider
features/terms and jurisdiction-specific rights require current evidence.

## Generate bounded, inspectable assets

Confirm an actual audio tool, authorized inputs, intended use and permitted cost.
Without one, return a labeled production/generation specification, not a fictitious
WAV, listening report or model run. A provider documentation example does not
prove that the same provider is connected in the current environment.

Specify one asset's action/material, perspective, acoustic space, attack/body/tail,
approximate duration/handles, loop/one-shot, variation and unwanted content (e.g.
speech/music). These are requests, not guaranteed controls. Pin actual model and
settings in the registry; check current duration/format/voice capabilities rather
than embedding a provider's changing defaults into the skill.

On returned files inspect format, content and timing. Listen for wrong/repeated
events, missing transients, smeared attacks, watery/metallic modulation, unintended
speech/music, clipped tails, background changes, identity/accent drift, unstable
pitch, breaths and loop seams. A negative prompt cannot certify their absence.
If exact repetition or sync fails, isolate one-shots and edit/perform them against
anchors rather than regenerating a long complex scene indefinitely. One revised
attempt after diagnosis is a reasonable local default; respect the user's budget
and record failure before choosing a recorded/library/manual alternative.

AI music/voice/SFX outputs need the same rights, content and acoustic checks as
other assets. A seed doesn't prove reproducibility; generative layers may contain
baked music/speech that makes clean stems or M&E impossible. Do not promise stem
isolation from a composite track without inspecting the actual separation result.

## Provenance and rights

For each used/temporary asset retain stable ID/version/take, actual locator/hash
if available, creator/performer/source URL, acquisition date, source type,
license/release/consent evidence locator, allowed use (project, media, commercial
scope, territory, term, adaptation/training/synthesis), required attribution,
restrictions, clearance owner and status. For generated material add input asset
IDs/permissions, provider/model/settings/text revision and generation date/result
ID. Do not store secret tokens or publicize private consent records in packets.

Check composition and recording permissions separately for reused music. A
subscription, public upload, “royalty-free” label, AI generation or available
download is not complete clearance evidence. The U.S. Copyright Office's sync/
master explanation is U.S.-specific; do not claim it settles Thai or worldwide
law, a particular license, or AI authorship. Route uncertain clearance to the
authorized rights owner; continue design with a clearly marked placeholder or
an authorized replacement and keep the unresolved asset out of release.

For custom/cloned voice, document the speaker's explicit authorization covering
the actual synthesis/training/languages/use, plus provider requirements. A sample
or permission to record one line is insufficient evidence for cloning new lines.
If scope is missing, do not train/clone; offer a licensed stock voice or performer
brief. Record expiration/revocation and affected assets; escalate deletion/reuse
handling to the agreement owner rather than inventing contractual remedies.
Consent and distribution rights are distinct checks; approval of a sound mix
does not clear either.

## Captions and accessible listening

Plan access from spotting, including meaningful non-speech sounds, speaker
identification, spoken content, language and timing. Same-language captions and
translated dialogue subtitles may serve different needs; a translation alone
does not document all important audio information. Treat automatic transcripts
as drafts until compared with the actual chosen takes.

For a hidden-source clue use an accurate neutral description such as
“[three knocks behind the door]”; do not name the hidden character before the
story reveals identity. Do not erase useful information merely to preserve a
twist—convey what the hearing audience can perceive at that time. Accessibility
and story access should preserve equivalent knowledge opportunities.

Send cue-linked caption notes/timed text to the caption implementer or Editor;
verify speech, names, negations, meaningful sound labels, synchronization and
readability against the final version. Caption timing and player rendering need
real media/player inspection. A written note is not an implemented caption track.
Keep visual-information audio description separate from captions; coordinate
scripted gaps/mix space for an authorized description version when required.
Track transcripts and alternate mixes by locale/cut. Do not claim WCAG compliance
from an audio cue sheet or a single caption check.
