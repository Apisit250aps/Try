# Narrative listening

Use a listening design, not a compulsory layer list. Source basis: P01–P02 in
[notes](../research/notes.md); the procedures below are local craft synthesis.

## Choose what sound does

Write the intended change in audience attention/inference: “the empty room may
be occupied” is actionable; “cinematic and immersive” is too vague. For each
important beat choose a lead layer, supporting space and what to omit. Match
to Director intent; a sound may reveal information the picture withholds.

Distinguish source in the story world (diegetic), score/comment outside it
(non-diegetic), and subjective/internal experience. On/offscreen is a separate
axis: an unseen door in the same scene is still diegetic. A prerecorded source
can be presented as present action. If source identity is deliberately ambiguous,
record what the audience can infer now and when confirmation becomes allowed.

Choose the listener: world observer, character, memory or a deliberate shift.
Specify near/far, obstruction, direct/reverberant balance, movement and acoustic
space before pan. A close-up need not make a distant machine sound close. Preserve
world direction across cuts even when screen composition reverses; subjective
deviation needs a narrative reason and an exit back to the established space.

## Motifs, silence, offscreen and bridges

- Motif: identify its narrative association, recognizable rhythm/timbre,
  permitted variation and withheld features. Track recurrence by cue/episode
  and character/audience knowledge; do not reduce every character to a jingle.
- Silence: specify which layers stop, remain or recede, the trigger and return.
  Relative quiet, subjective withdrawal and digital zero have different effects.
  Preserve intentional zero if requested; don't normalize a silent cue upward.
  An absent file is not deliberate silence. Test whether the beat still reads.
- Offscreen: name world location, source uncertainty, timing and reaction.
  If the culprit must remain unknown, avoid recognizable voice, signature motif
  or caption naming them. Provide spatial clues that survive mono if essential.
- Bridges: incoming audio before picture (J-cut) or outgoing audio continuing
  after picture (L-cut) can connect or misdirect. Specify both scene IDs, which
  source time/world it belongs to, overlap and reveal risk. Editor owns cut
  implementation. A bridge must not accidentally imply continuous place/time.

Counterexample: “Add scary music and a scream to every empty corridor.” It may
announce danger before the story permits it. Better: compare a distant uncertain
mechanical pulse with a score-led alternative; state which inference each invites
and check the selected version with picture and captions.

## Formats and continuity

Short-form: establish one readable listening priority quickly; avoid filling every
transition with a whoosh. Protect speech/clue space, mobile audibility and muted
caption comprehension. A hook is a design hypothesis, not a retention result.

Animation: choose material/weight and stylization rules with Artist/Director;
sync selected story actions, not every motion automatically. Build accents around
performance and holds. Separate voice takes and viseme timing from picture lock.

Series: maintain voice/model/locale revisions, location room signatures, recurring
prop textures, motif states, diegesis and episode loudness profiles. Carry scene
entry/exit state (door open, engine on, storm strength) across cuts. For time jumps,
record intentional discontinuity. A recut invalidates affected cue/caption timing
and sync evidence; reuse of an asset does not prove continuity of its placement.
