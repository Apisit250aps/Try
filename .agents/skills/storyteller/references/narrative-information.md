# Audience access, time and inference

Contents: POV/focalization; information effects; clues/payoffs; time; pacing.
Keep these choices at narrative level. A permission to show a fact is not a
prescribed close-up or edit. Source IDs resolve in the [registry](../research/sources.md).

## Who speaks, perceives, knows and believes?

Separate narrator/voice (“who tells?”) from focalization (selection/restriction of
information) and the person whose experience matters. First/third person alone
does not determine access to minds. Niederhoff discusses competing definitions
and zero/internal/external focalization ([S06](../research/sources.md#s06)); use
terms to clarify decisions, not to enforce a taxonomy on every medium.

| Access choice | When / why | How / trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restricted to a character | Share uncertainty, limited interpretation or intimacy. | Establish what they can perceive/learn. A hidden motive can remain uncertain; narrating another's private thought without transition breaks a strict restriction. |
| External/observable | Preserve opacity, enable a silent story or invite inference. | Supply behavior, objects and consequences. “She remembers the promise” is production intention unless there is evidence the audience can read. |
| Broad/omniscient | Compare people, worlds, times or collective experience. | Establish the breadth and a coherent organizing voice; avoid treating every shift as an error. Intimacy may be diluted if access changes without purpose. |
| Multiple perspectives | Contrast testimony, unequal knowledge or values. | Mark whose access governs each section and what changes between accounts. Retelling the same events must add a new relation, not just repeat exposition. |
| Unreliable account | Explore misinterpretation, selective reporting or value conflict. | Plant a gap between account and evidence. Decide what is unreliable: fact, interpretation or evaluation; not simply “the person is mentally ill” ([S07](../research/sources.md#s07)). |

Use a knowledge ledger for mysteries, secrets and long continuity:

| Fact / event | True state / certainty | Character knows or believes / learned at | Audience access / first shown | Effect / later update |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not conflate false belief with a retcon. A narrator's assertion is not
automatically world truth; a dream is not automatically a historical event.
For nonlinear stories, order knowledge by the moment a character acts, not by
where a flashback appears to the audience.

## Suspense, mystery/curiosity and surprise

These are working distinctions, not mutually exclusive genres. Plot scholarship
connects them to readers' evolving expectations ([S05](../research/sources.md#s05));
the mystery practitioners themselves disagree on genre boundaries ([S16](../research/sources.md#s16)).

- **Suspense:** a consequential future outcome is uncertain. Give enough concern,
  possible outcomes and progress for anticipation. Audience superiority is one
  method, not a necessary definition: a person and reader can fear together.
- **Mystery/curiosity:** an acknowledged gap asks what happened, how, who or why.
  Give a tractable question and evidence that changes plausible explanations.
- **Surprise:** a development violates a prepared expectation. Test whether it
  deepens prior meaning or only contradicts information arbitrarily.

Same underlying fact, different access: a ferry leaves with a bag uncollected.
Show the owner racing toward it: anticipation of missing the ferry. Show a strange
claim ticket: curiosity about the owner. Reveal it belongs to the clerk herself:
surprise, earned only if her relationship to it has been prepared. No camera
choice is needed to specify these narrative functions.

## Fair-play mystery and setup/payoff

For a promised solvable puzzle, work backward from truth and forward from the
investigation. Establish relevant rules/capabilities, opportunity, evidence,
competing interpretations and why the solver updates. All indispensable evidence
must be available before the solution; this need not make the answer obvious
([S16](../research/sources.md#s16)). The detective may be wrong; the narration must
honor its access contract. A no-supernatural premise cannot gain an unseen
teleportation power at the reveal.

Use one compact ledger (part of the outline template):

| Setup/clue ID | Actual evidence / established rule | First available beat | Plausible initial meaning | Payoff / inference beat | Dependency / status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Run a rival-solution check: could each alternative still fit every disclosed fact?
If yes, add a discriminating earlier clue, narrow the claimed deduction, or make
the ending explicitly probabilistic. A confession may confirm evidence; it should
not substitute for reasoning if a deduction was promised. Do not mark “fair-play
passed” merely because a clue table exists.

Red herrings should arise from true observations, limited inference or motivated
misleading acts. Pay off their real meaning where the false trail matters.
Counterexample: objectively asserting a broken clock was accurate, then calling it
unreliable at the end without prior basis, is changing evidence to fit the answer.

Setup/payoff also applies outside puzzles: skills, promises, limits, rituals,
relationships and objects. Show sufficient usable capacity before a decisive use.
Foreshadowing creates anticipation or retrospective coherence; a motif can instead
create resonance with no mechanical payoff. Do not force every repeated cup to
be a weapon. A too-prominent clue can spoil a puzzle; disguising it in meaningful
activity preserves availability without a sign saying “remember this.”

## Narrative time

Distinguish chronology/story time, discourse/presentation and the time of telling.
Track order, duration and frequency ([S17](../research/sources.md#s17)).

- **Order:** choose chronological, flashback, flash-forward, parallel or other
  arrangement for an identifiable effect. Give anchors when disorientation is
  not the intended experience. Reveal a cause later without letting a character
  use knowledge they have not acquired at the earlier story time.
- **Duration:** expand a decision, compress routine, pause for observation, or
  omit an interval. A quiet interval can change interpretation. An unmarked
  omission of the only decisive action may evade the promised payoff.
- **Frequency:** tell a single event once; repeat it with changed access; or
  summarize recurring activity. Each recurrence should support a function rather
  than automatically count as a new event in the continuity ledger.

Example: show the clerk returning a letter, then the earlier promise that made
it costly. This may reframe an apparently generous act as betrayal. Counterexample:
she names its contents before opening it in story time without an established
route to that knowledge. An intentionally impossible narrator/time system is
possible, but establish that contract instead of using it to excuse a mistake.

## Emotional progression and pacing

At outline level track **audience question/expectation → new evidence/action →
changed interpretation or investment → space to register it**. Distinguish event
rate, information density, tension and perceived progress. Cutting every reaction
can make a faster sequence less emotionally legible. Increasing danger in every
beat can flatten contrast.

When a middle sags, diagnose before shortening: repeated attempts with identical
costs? no change in knowledge? an unresolved motive? too many unrelated promises?
Merge or transform redundant functions. Preserve a slow beat if it changes how
the next action is understood. Conversely, length without new perception does
not become contemplation merely by description.

Break before an answer to sustain anticipation; break after it to allow reaction.
Both have costs, and the return should justify the delay ([S18](../research/sources.md#s18)).
These are narrative grouping decisions; exact shots, performance pauses and final
cut rhythm belong to production collaborators. For prose, word-count pacing and
sentence texture may be authored when requested, without pretending a page count
proves screen duration. Revisit intended emotion after changes to information:
an early reveal can turn a sad reunion into an uneasy one even with identical events.
