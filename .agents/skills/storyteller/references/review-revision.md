# Narrative review, revision and evidence

This is a local rubric. Assess the actual delivered material against its brief;
do not reward extra templates or force a preferred narrative ideology.

## Review dimensions

For each applicable dimension use 0 absent/contradictory, 1 major problem,
2 usable with a stated minor issue, 3 meets the brief on inspected evidence.
Use U for uninspected and N/A for irrelevant. Do not average away hard failures.

| Dimension | Observable test |
| --- | --- |
| Intent and specificity | Does a particular experience emerge? Are materially different options actually different? Does the ending honor the stated genre/experience promise? |
| Organizing logic | Are choices/events motivated and consequential, or are associations/patterns legible for the chosen form? Can a weak link be located? |
| Character and relationship | Do available knowledge, motive, capability and cost support key choices? Does the relationship change through exchanges? Witness-centered stories are judged by their intended function. |
| Information and time | Are POV/access, chronology and disclosure coherent? Can promised puzzle answers be inferred from prior evidence? Are important alternatives excluded or acknowledged? |
| Emotion, rhythm and ending | What changes in investment or perception? Does the reader have enough evidence to register it? Are major setups resolved, reinterpreted or deliberately carried? |
| Continuity and revision | Are canon locks, IDs/versions, world limits, object custody and knowledge preserved? Were affected downstream records checked or explicitly marked pending? |
| Deliverable and evidence | Is the requested story material actually present? Are source limits, assumptions, untested impact and approval status honest? Are collaboration boundaries usable? |

Hard failures: unauthorized canon revision; contradiction that breaks an essential
deduction/choice; screenplay/shot list replacing a requested narrative outline;
falsely claimed source access, audience test or approval; missing requested ending;
or a critical factual/cultural dependency presented as verified without evidence.
A failing one-off can remain a useful draft but cannot be reported as accepted.
A serious issue in one subdeliverable need not prevent unrelated work.

An outline can be reviewed as an outline. It cannot receive an observed-viewer
emotion or screen-readability pass. Review-only outputs should locate problems
and propose corrections without silently replacing the submitted work.

## Revision workflow

1. Restate the requested change and preserved invariants, using the user's actual
   authorization. Identify whether the note concerns an event, motivation, access,
   interpretation, style or factual dependency.
2. Diagnose the earliest cause. “The ending feels sudden” may come from missing
   capacity, a contradicted motive, absent setup or insufficient reaction, each
   needing a different fix. Do not simply add more exposition at the end.
3. If useful, compare a small structural change against a larger one with costs.
   Preserve the brief's chosen quietness or ambiguity unless revision changes it.
4. Produce changed material, not only revision advice when an edit was requested.
   Preserve prior versions; mark obsolete beats as superseded rather than reusing
   their IDs for unrelated events.
5. Trace dependencies: rule/fact → motive and knowledge → action → clue/payoff →
   ending → affected bible, episode outline, script/art packet. Recheck both
   story-time and presentation order. Classify outputs as updated, valid or pending.
6. Re-evaluate the changed function and neighboring constraints. Report observed
   correction plus residual uncertainty, not “fixed” because the note was restated.

Example: a culprit is “deduced” from access, but all suspects had access. Repair
the earlier evidence so it discriminates, or narrow the final claim; a longer
detective speech cannot solve the logic gap. Counterexample: secretly denying
another suspect access after the fact changes canon to make the answer work.

## Evaluation protocol

Use the raw [scenarios](../tests/scenarios.md) with a copied skill and minimum
inputs. Save actual generated answers; do not label expected outputs as runs.
Record executor, context isolation, loaded guidance, tool access, revisions and
review method. A same-author exercise can demonstrate authored outcomes and
reveal inconsistencies, but is not independent forward-testing or measured
reliability. Repeat changed cases if a discovered defect changes behavior.

Judge both requested output and rejected shortcuts: does the silent story depend
on captions? Could a rival still solve the mystery? Does a prequel remember a later
event? Did a revision propagate a removed capability? Is a contemplative work
silently converted into a pursuit? Save evidence even when it fails.

Audience comprehension/emotion needs actual readers/viewers and a reported method;
screen timing needs performed/animatic evidence; interoperability with future
skills needs real receiver execution. Packaging checks establish none of these.
