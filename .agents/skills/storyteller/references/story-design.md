# Story design and structural judgment

Contents: concept and genre; causality and stakes; structure selection; endings;
originality. Source IDs link to the [registry](../research/sources.md).
Decision tests and examples below are local synthesis, not empirical laws.

## Concept before a formula

Distinguish subject (grief), premise (a household keeps setting a place for an
absent member), and thematic inquiry (when does preserving a ritual prevent living?).
A theme can remain a question or contradiction. Test it through choices and
consequences, not a final speech announcing the correct moral. Ribó's theme chapter
overview distinguishes persuasion from morally ambiguous representation ([S03](../research/sources.md#s03));
it is not a requirement that every story teach a lesson.

For a usable premise identify a particular situation, point of attention and
generative change or pattern. Ask what this premise makes possible that another
setting/cast would not. For development, compare alternatives along a real axis:
whose experience, what changes, what is disclosed, or how the ending reframes it.
Changing the protagonist's name is not an alternative story.

**Logline:** compress the central experience into one or two sentences. A pursuit
can state person + disruption + difficult action + consequential stakes. A quiet
story can state person/place + recurring pattern + discovery + altered meaning.
No villain or deadline is required just to fit a sentence template. A logline may
withhold a reveal for pitching; a development synopsis normally tells the ending.
Label which kind is being delivered.

Example: “A retiring ferry clerk who secretly keeps passengers' unclaimed letters
must decide which to return when her successor starts an official lost-property
register.” This creates action and a privacy/belonging dilemma. Counterexample:
“A woman learns that love is everything” names a conclusion but supplies no story.

## Genre is a negotiated expectation

Use audience, medium, period and the brief to define the actual promise. Genre
can guide interest without dictating every event ([S04](../research/sources.md#s04)).
For a hybrid choose which promise governs the ending and which supplies texture.
Do not treat practitioner categories as a universally agreed taxonomy.

| Intended mode | Working promise / useful test | Trade-off or counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fair-play mystery | An answer can be inferred from evidence disclosed before the solution; test rival explanations. | A final secret twin with no prior trace adds surprise but breaks this puzzle promise. |
| Suspense/thriller | A consequential outcome feels uncertain; track threat and options. | Withholding the threat may create curiosity instead of anticipation. Hybrids can use both. |
| Relationship drama | Competing needs produce consequential exchanges, refusals or care; both people have perspectives. | One explanatory apology cannot erase repeated harm without further choices. |
| Genre romance | If the brief invokes its optimistic relationship-ending convention, make that the central payoff and clarify exceptions. | A tragic love story can be excellent but should not be sold under a contrary ending promise. This is a working convention, not a researched market claim. |
| Comedy | Establish a comic pattern or perspective; vary, escalate or recontextualize it with consequences. | Random absurd events may fit a surreal brief, but are not automatically a developed comic pattern. |
| Horror/speculative | Establish the kind of unease, wonder or possibility and its relevant constraints. | Explaining every unknown may destroy dread; unexplained convenient powers may destroy fairness. |
| Contemplative/associative | Reward attention through pattern, texture, contrast or changed perception. | Adding a chase to “fix the lack of conflict” can violate the intended experience. |

Use relevant comparable works analytically when known or actually inspected:
what promise, mechanism and difference? Do not fabricate viewership, current
market demand, source scenes or proof of originality.

## Plot, causality, conflict and stakes

Plot concerns arranging events and enabling readers to connect motivation and
consequence; theory also recognizes dynamic reader inference ([S05](../research/sources.md#s05)).
Do not equate chronological adjacency with causality.

For consequential plots, trace **condition → motive/pressure → choice/action →
changed state → next available choices**. Distinguish a necessary condition from
a sufficient cause. Coincidence can start trouble; repeated coincidence that
removes costs weakens the promised agency. An accident can end a tragedy if its
contingency is the subject, rather than an unnoticed repair for missing logic.

Test a link by deletion: if the previous beat vanished, why would the next still
happen? Either justify the link, merge redundancy, or label an intentional
parallel/associative connection. A montage of seasonal routines needs a pattern
of variation, not a fabricated chain of people causing the seasons.

Conflict can arise between legitimate desires, values, obligations, environments,
institutions or a person's incompatible commitments. It need not be violence or
a morally evil antagonist. State the particular obstacle and available response;
“society opposes her” is insufficient without a mechanism relevant to this story.

Stakes are what matters, to whom, and what a choice, delay or change costs.
Personal trust can matter more than a city-wide threat. Show the threatened value
before threatening it. Escalation may mean narrowing options, changing allegiance,
revealing responsibility or increasing intimacy rather than increasing body count.
In a contemplative work, describe investment and change in attention without
pretending there is a high-risk conflict.

## Choose a structure by its job

For an open brief first identify its engine; then choose one form or a justified
hybrid. State what it clarifies and what it might distort. Do not run every story
through every framework, allocate compulsory percentages, or invent universal
brain/culture rules. Structures below are lenses, not interchangeable labels.

| Lens | When / why | How to use | Trade-offs / counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Three acts | A central undertaking changes direction and culminates in a consequential outcome. Useful for locating commitment and consequence. | Setup establishes meaningful conditions; a turn commits the undertaking; development changes tactics/costs; a further turn enables or forces the decisive action; resolution makes its consequences legible. | Not three equally long blocks. Adding a “midpoint disaster” without causal need is padding. Field's screenplay paradigm is one specific version, not all narrative ([S11](../research/sources.md#s11)). |
| Story Circle | A character's excursion out of a familiar condition returns with a changed relationship to it. Useful for compact goal/cost/return checks. | Familiar self → want → unfamiliar situation → adaptation → attainment → cost → return → change. Check the contrast at return; stages may share a beat. | Do not force a literal homecoming, reward, or reset after irreversible loss. Eight-step details are backed here by a labeled professional secondary explanation; the original essay was unavailable ([S12](../research/sources.md#s12)). |
| Hero's Journey | A threshold experience and integration are the premise's concern. Useful for a transformation quest and its social return. | Consider departure/separation, initiation and return; select relevant variations, not a compulsory seventeen-stage itinerary. | Campbell's framework and later adaptations differ. It is not proof of a universal narrative or a mandate for chosen-one exceptionalism ([S13](../research/sources.md#s13)). |
| Kishōtenketsu | Development and contrast change the meaning of what came before. Useful for discovery, observation or reframing. | Ki introduces; shō develops a coherent field/pattern; ten introduces a connected contrast or shift; ketsu brings the relation into view. | Ten need not be a shocking secret. Conflict may occur; it is not required as the engine. Do not call all Asian stories conflict-free or treat one paper's readings as a universal cultural law ([S14](../research/sources.md#s14)). |
| Braided/ensemble | Interdependence or contrasting accounts is the subject. | Give each strand a local interest and distinct access; track intersection points and what their adjacency changes. | More strands cost attention; if no strand changes another's meaning, juxtaposition may feel arbitrary. |
| Cyclical, episodic vignette, associative or carrier-oriented | Care, routine, community or accumulated experience matters more than conquest. | Organize repetitions, objects, encounters or perceptions around a discernible relation; vary them; close on a changed relation or deliberate continuation. | A list is not automatically meaningful accumulation. Le Guin's official book description supports a non-heroic alternative, but the full essay was not read here ([S15](../research/sources.md#s15)). |
| Nonlinear/discovery-led | Learning the order of events is part of their meaning. | Make a chronology first, then a disclosure order; assign a purpose to each displacement. | Concealing temporal coordinates can overwhelm rather than intrigue; preserve enough orientation for the intended inference. |

Illustration using the ferry clerk premise: a three-act version follows her
decision to return a letter and its consequences; a circle follows a delivery trip
that changes what she considers “keeping safe”; a kishōtenketsu version develops
unclaimed objects, then reveals that passengers have also kept things for her.
The last version changes the question rather than merely moving an act break.

Other forms can be chosen without these names. Specify the organizing principle
and test its function. A story can contain process, perception and modest changes
without an exceptional event ([S08](../research/sources.md#s08)).

## Endings and emotional consequence

List what was promised: event outcome, question, relationship, self-understanding,
pattern or experience. Decide which to resolve, transform, deliberately leave open,
or carry forward. Open outcome can coexist with emotional closure. A cliffhanger
should not replace every promised local payoff.

Work backward from an ending as a hypothesis: what capability, knowledge or
relationship must already exist? Plant that without announcing the solution.
Also work forward: would this character choose it given what happened? If the
two paths disagree, revise the setup, choice or ending rather than forcing it.

An earned reversal changes interpretation while preserving established evidence.
An earned quiet ending can repeat an action with a changed meaning. Avoid equating
sadness with depth, reconciliation with forgiveness, or death with completion.
Example: the clerk lets a passenger choose whether to accept a letter, paying off
her shift from possession to custodianship. Counterexample: a late inheritance
solves every conflict while her prior choices cease to matter.

## Originality as design work

Find specificity in pressure, viewpoint, relationships, place and consequence.
Change a mechanism, not only surface nouns. Compare the closest known resemblance:
what is shared (trope/idea), what is distinct (sequence, expression, choice and
meaning)? This is a bounded comparison, not a global novelty search or clearance.
The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes ideas from expression ([S22](../research/sources.md#s22));
that does not settle adaptation rights, other jurisdictions or community protocols.
Record reference roles; never relabel borrowed passages as original work.
