# Storyteller source registry

Research date: **2026-11-25**. “Read” below means the identified public text or
selected sections, not a whole book/course/video. Entries distinguish full-text
sections, publisher overviews, metadata and secondary explanation. No source
text, diagrams or questionnaires are redistributed. These sources inform craft;
they do not prove that this skill produces professional-quality stories.

## S01

**Ignasi Ribó, Prose Fiction (2019), chapter 2: Plot.** Textbook, Open Book Publishers;
author identified by publisher as a Mae Fah Luang University lecturer.
[Publisher chapter overview](https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0187/chapters/10.11647/obp.0187.02).
Read the public overview, pp.17–31 locator and metadata, **not the chapter body**.
It identifies story/plot, emplotment, conflict and suspense/surprise as chapter
topics. Detailed plot claims in this skill rely on S05 instead. Chapter PDF
returned 405; OpenEdition access presented a bot challenge. No full-book claim.

## S02

**Ribó, chapter 4: Characterisation.**
[Publisher overview](https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0187/chapters/10.11647/obp.0187.04).
Read overview and metadata, chapter locator pp.47–63; not the body (PDF 405).
Supports distinguishing degree of individuation from development (flat/round
versus static/dynamic) and direct/indirect representation. Does not supply a
validated psychology, motivation model or compulsory transformation arc.

## S03

**Ribó, chapter 7: Theme.**
[Publisher overview](https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0187/chapters/10.11647/obp.0187.07).
Read overview/metadata, pp.95–108 locator, not chapter body (PDF 405).
Identifies theme, ideology, identity/alterity and the distinction between moral
persuasion and ambiguous representation. The skill's thematic-question and
choice tests are local synthesis, not quotations from the unread chapter.

## S04

**The Open University, Start writing fiction.** University teaching course.
[Public printable course](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/content-section-3.3/?printable=1).
Read selected text on character/plot and sections 3.1–3.3 on genre/expectations.
Supports character-generated plot and genre as a reader/writer expectation;
acknowledges predictability and hybrid borrowing. It is introductory teaching,
not a current genre-market study or an argument that all stories require conflict.

## S05

**Karin Kukkonen, “Plot” (2014), Living Handbook of Narratology, Hamburg archive.**
Scholarly reference authored by a researcher.
[Full-text entry](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/115.html).
Read Definition, Explication and selected Global Structure/Progressive Structuration
sections. Plot can be considered global pattern, developing reader inference or
authorial design. Causal/temporal relations and expectation interact. The entry
discusses disagreements; it does not endorse one complete taxonomy or universal
formula. The deletion test in this skill is an authoring heuristic, not its rule.

## S06

**Burkhard Niederhoff, “Focalization” (2011, revised 2013), LHN.**
[Full-text entry](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/18.html).
Read Definition and Explication, including Genette's distinctions and qualifications
about perspective/access. Supports separating information restriction from a
simple first/third-person label. Definitions remain debated; the skill's knowledge
ledger is a practical adaptation, not a narratological standard or camera plan.

## S07

**Dan Shen, “Unreliability” (2011, revised 2013), LHN.**
[Full-text entry](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/66.html).
Read Definition, Explication and Features/Causes. Reporting, interpreting and
evaluating can diverge, and misreporting differs from underreporting. Signals and
reader inference matter. The article surveys historical terminology, including
dated pathological labels; these are not adopted as character-design categories.
Unreliability is not inferred from demographic or diagnostic identity.

## S08

**Peter Hühn, “Event and Eventfulness” (2011, revised 2013), LHN.**
[Full-text entry](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/39.html).
Read Definition/Explication and the discussion of context and tellability.
Distinguishes changes of state from contextually significant exceptional events.
Supports evaluating modest/process-oriented narratives without demanding dramatic
reversal. Does not mean any list of observations is automatically effective fiction.

## S09

**Jean-Marie Schaeffer, “Fictional vs. Factual Narration” (2012, revised 2013), LHN.**
[Full-text entry](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/56.html).
Read Definition, Validity of the Fact/Fiction Opposition, and Fact and Fiction,
Narrative and Non-narrative. Discusses several competing distinctions and the
consequences of confusing truth claims with make-believe. Supports labeling
invention/claim status. Does not verify a particular historical, cultural, medical
or legal fact used in a story.

## S10

**Marie-Laure Ryan, “Possible Worlds” (2012, revised 2013), LHN.**
[Full-text entry](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/54.html).
Read Definition and selected discussion of fictional incompleteness, Doležel's
modal systems and character/reader possible worlds. Supports differentiating world
truth, permissions, values and beliefs, leaving some gaps unspecified. The skill's
world-rule/knowledge tables are local operational tools, not formal modal logic.

## S11

**Syd Field, The Paradigm Worksheet (2013 web page); Valerie Woods, Screenwriting
Form and Freedom (2018).** Creator's official site; primary framework account and
an instructor's own workshop reflection.
[Field text](https://sydfield.com/syd_resources/the-paradigm-worksheet/),
[Woods text](https://sydfield.com/guest-blog/screenwriting-form-and-freedomby-valerie-woods/).
Read their public text on beginning/turns/ending, form, page counts and flexibility.
Supports using turns as anchors without rigid timing. These are screenplay craft
perspectives, not proof of a structure shared by all cultures or all literature.
No full paid book or embedded audio/video consumed.

## S12

**Dan Harmon, The Story Circle — Adult Swim official clip page; StudioBinder,
Storytelling Guide: Dan Harmon's Story Circle Method Explained (2025).**
[Official page](https://www.adultswim.com/videos/rick-and-morty/the-story-circle),
[Professional secondary explanation](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/dan-harmon-story-circle/).
Official page opened: only description/attribution was available in extracted
text; **video not watched**. Read StudioBinder's definition and eight-step section.
Eight-step details therefore have secondary support in this batch. The original
Channel 101 essay mirror failed to fetch. Reject StudioBinder's “foolproof” and
universal-language rhetoric; the local skill treats the circle as optional.

## S13

**Joseph Campbell Foundation, Joseph Campbell and the Hero's Journey.** Author's
foundation and archive-based exposition.
[Source](https://www.jcf.org/learn/joseph-campbell-heros-journey).
Read separation/initiation/return and the discussion of stages versus variations,
including the warning against including every possible alternative. Supports
selecting relevant variations, not mandatory seventeen steps. Foundation advocacy
does not independently establish cross-cultural universality or efficacy.
Campbell's complete book and later Vogler adaptations were not read here.

## S14

**Gabriel Oh, “Kishōtenketsu and its potential applications to prose writing,”
TEXT 29(2), October 2025; LASALLE College of the Arts author affiliation.**
Research article on the journal's own platform.
[Article record](https://textjournal.scholasticahq.com/article/146391-kishotenketsu-and-its-potential-applications-to-prose-writing),
[19-page full-text PDF](https://textjournal.scholasticahq.com/api/v1/articles/146391-kishotenketsu-and-its-potential-applications-to-prose-writing.pdf).
Read abstract and selected sections pp.2–7 on four parts, contrast, noncentral
conflict, passive protagonists and cycles. The article's historical claims and
examples sometimes rely on secondary popular writing. Treat its English-language
prose application as one argument, not an exhaustive East Asian poetics or a
simple “West has conflict / East has none” rule. The skill rejects that binary.

## S15

**Ursula K. Le Guin official site, The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction.**
[Book description](https://www.ursulakleguin.com/the-carrier-bag-theory-of-fiction).
Read the short publication description and supplement note, **not the essay**.
It presents a challenge to hero-centered, linear-progressive storytelling. Supports
considering a non-heroic alternative, not a detailed reconstruction of Le Guin's
argument or a formula named “carrier bag structure.” Routine/collection exercises
in this skill are explicitly local authoring choices.

## S16

**Writing Excuses 9.43: Writing Mysteries (2014), practitioners with Mette Ivie
Harrison and J. R. Johannson.**
[Public episode and transcript](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-9-43-writing-mysteries/).
Read transcript sections on thriller/mystery distinctions, puzzle presentation and
fair play. Supports providing the audience the information needed to solve a
promised puzzle. Speakers disagree on genre boundaries; transcript warns some
speaker attribution may be wrong. No rigid thriller=mystery taxonomy or exact
speaker quotation is adopted. Rival-solution checking is local synthesis.

## S17

**Michael Scheffel, Antonius Weixler and Lukas Werner, “Time” (2013, revised 2014), LHN.**
[Full-text entry](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/106.html).
Read Definition/Explication and Story/Discourse/Narrating Time, especially order,
duration and frequency. Supports distinguishing chronology, presentation and
temporal relations. The authors also discuss anomalies and limits of classical
models. The skill's beat tables are not claims that all narratives have a single
reconstructible Newtonian chronology.

## S18

**Writing Excuses 10.32: How Do I Control the Speed of the Story? (2015).**
[Episode transcript](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-10-32-how-do-i-control-the-speed-of-the-story/).
Read introduction and transcript discussion of breaks, revelation and time for
reaction. Supports contrasting anticipation with processing time and checking
whether delayed answers reward readers. Mechanical punctuation metaphors are
not carried into runtime estimates or treated as scientific timing rules.

## S19

**Patricia C. Wrede, Fantasy Worldbuilding Questions (©1996), author's own site.**
[Introduction and topic index](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions/).
Read introduction and topic list. Explicitly rejects answering every question as a
condition of beginning/finishing fiction. Supports selective worldbuilding. The
SFWA archive routes were indexed but failed direct opens; the author-hosted route
is used instead. The copyrighted questionnaire is not copied into the package.

## S20

**Wrede, Magic and Magicians; Physical and Historical Features.** Primary craft
question sets on the author's site.
[Magic](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-magic-and-magicians/),
[Physical/history](https://pcwrede.com/pcw-wp/fantasy-worldbuilding-questions-physical-and-historical-features/).
Read selected Rules of Magic questions and climate/geography topic sections;
direct navigation from the index redirected to these canonical routes. Supports
testing limits, costs and practical effects on movement/life. Dated assumptions
in examples are not endorsed; questions are prompts, not cultural/scientific facts.

## S21

**Creative Australia / Dr Terri Janke, Protocols for using First Nations cultural
and intellectual property in the arts.** National arts body's public resource page.
[Landing page](https://creative.gov.au/first-nations-arts/protocols-for-using-first-nations-cultural-and-intellectual-property-in-the-arts).
Read Background and the explanation of cultural heritage/self-determination and
funding context. Linked full PDF timed out; it is **not counted as read**. Supports
recognizing community authority and meaningful collaboration. It does not settle
another community's permissions or current law. Specific release decisions need
the applicable full guidance and relevant community input.

## S22

**U.S. Copyright Office, What Does Copyright Protect?** Owning public institution.
[FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html).
Read protection, idea and diary/ownership sections. Supports distinguishing ideas
from expression and possession from rights ownership. Scope is U.S. general
information, not a legal opinion on a particular output, global clearance,
plagiarism detection or proof that an AI-created text is copyrightable.

## S23

**Writing Excuses 12.32: Structuring a Short Piece (2017).** Practitioner account.
[Episode and transcript](https://writingexcuses.com/12-32-structuring-a-short-piece/).
Read MACE discussion, short/flash thread limits and nested openings/closures.
Supports using thread count to diagnose scope; attribution to Card's MICE and
Kowal's adaptation is retained. “One or two threads” is a useful tendency, not a
hard cap or a formula for seconds of finished film. Embedded fiction was not
read or reproduced.

## S24

**Jason Mittell, Middlebury College, Serial Characterization and Inferred
Interiority (2011), In Media Res / MediaCommons.** Scholar's own analytical note.
[Text](https://mediacommons.org/imr/2011/11/30/serial-characterization-and-inferred-interiority).
Read Curator's Note and example analysis, not the embedded clip. Supports how
accumulated serial knowledge can inform inference about interiority. It analyzes
one work; it is not audience experiment data or a complete series-design manual.
Local episode state/continuity contracts are synthesis, not imported TV standards.

## S25

**ScreenSkills, Storyboard artist (animation).** Industry skills body.
[Job profile](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/animation/pre-production/storyboard-artist/).
Read duties, skills and collaborators. Storyboards translate script/directorial
vision into panels planning shots and continuity. Supports keeping written story
requirements distinct from delivered boards. Actual roles overlap across studios;
the project's Storyteller/Artist/Cinematographer split is a local agreement.

## Access failures and excluded candidates

Screen Australia's Story Documents – Drama and Pathways & Protocols pages/PDFs
returned 403 or inaccessible routes after useful search excerpts. Their indexed
summaries were inspected for discovery only; **the full guides are not accepted
evidence**. SPJ ethics page returned 403 and is excluded. Unrelated search hits,
university theses quoting popular framework lists, commercial course listings
and uninspected videos are not counted as research coverage.

The initial Python download attempt failed certificate validation; an OS curl
retry confirmed the publisher PDF 405. No certificate bypass, access challenge
evasion or private material retrieval was used. Source availability is checked
only for this research date, not guaranteed for future installations.
