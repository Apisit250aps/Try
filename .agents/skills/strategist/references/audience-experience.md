# Ethical attention, packaging and retention hypotheses

P02–P05/P09 in [notes](../research/notes.md). Behavioral research motivates
hypotheses; it does not specify platform ranking weights or universal cut timing.

## Promise, curiosity, payoff

Use an intelligible question or situation, enough context to care, and a payoff
the work actually delivers. Information-gap theory (S18) motivates asking what
the viewer knows and wants to learn; vague withholding can instead confuse.
Define the knowledge gap, why it matters, where it resolves, and what must remain
hidden for later episodes. A cliffhanger can complete a local promise while
keeping a larger question open; mark those promises separately.

Weak hook: “Episode 4.” Better proposed packaging: “Who keeps fixing the office
printer?” if this episode explores that question. Misleading: “The printer nearly
killed him” for a harmless paper jam. Reject the false promise; offer clearer
stakes from actual content. Never use fabricated urgency, fake testimonials,
engagement bait, humiliating stereotypes or outrage farming to exploit arousal.

Transportation research concerns absorption/beliefs in particular reading studies
(S19); it does not prove short-video retention. Sharing research includes news
emailing and experiments (S20), not a universal virality recipe. Translate to
testable possibilities—recognition, wonder, usefulness, emotional resolution—
within creative intent. A quiet or sad story can be successful on its own goals.

## Attention versus comprehension

Consider competing demands: fast captions, dialogue, new characters and visual
clues can require different processing at once. S21's limited-capacity television
model is a lens; the detailed pacing advice here is a design hypothesis, not a
quantified brain limit. Recommend a comprehension check before increasing cuts.
Retention is behavior, not direct attention, understanding, enjoyment or loyalty.
Rewatch can signal delight, confusion or looping; pair it with qualitative evidence.

Specify pacing goals as audience outcomes: “Understand the problem before the
reveal; preserve a readable reaction.” Ask Screenwriter/Editor/Director for
options. Do not mandate a cut every N seconds or rewrite dialogue in a strategy
packet. A cold open may improve entry clarity but spoil chronology; compare the
cost and preserve the Director's decision.

## Packaging/discoverability

Treat title, thumbnail/cover, first frame, caption and content as one consistent
promise, adapted to the surface. Brief Artist with function and truthful visual
elements; metadata should describe actual content in audience language. Use
relevant terms naturally, no keyword stuffing, unrelated trending tags or copied
competitor identity. Feed entry is different from registered-thumbnail click
entry; don't prescribe a CTR optimization for every Shorts/Reels exposure.
Native supported tests need current capability verification (S27).

Before changing packaging, ask: same topic, age, source and audience? Is the
promise visible and fulfilled? Low CTR + strong comparable retention suggests a
packaging/audience-match hypothesis; high CTR + early loss suggests promise or
entry-context mismatch, but neither proves cause (S04). Look at segment-level
curves and content, data quality and traffic shifts. Avoid “the algorithm hates
this.” Keep at least one alternative explanation and a retest criterion.
