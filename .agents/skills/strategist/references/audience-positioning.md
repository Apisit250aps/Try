# Audience, goals and positioning

P01/P06/P08/P12 in [notes](../research/notes.md). Frameworks below are decision
tools, not empirical proof of content-market fit.

## Make the audience need specific

Start with purpose: artistic reach, audience learning, community, qualified
conversion, loyalty or a viable business. Rank competing goals and name a cost
ceiling. Awareness can use eligible exposure/new viewers; engagement uses actual
consumption and qualitative comprehension; conversion needs a defined action and
attribution window; loyalty needs return behavior across episodes/time. A follow
does not establish loyalty and a view does not establish a sale.

Segment by circumstance and need before demographics: “a short reassuring break
after a frustrating work task” is testable; “global Gen Z loves animation” is an
unsupported generalization. Use JTBD as an interview lens (S25), distinguishing
functional progress, emotional relief and social expression. Document alternatives
the audience chooses (another creator, messaging a friend, no video) and barriers
(language, setup cost, time, sensory access). The same person can have different
needs in different sessions.

A persona includes evidence/sampling/window, observed behaviors/quotes with
privacy protection, inferred need, hypothetical attributes, contrary cases,
confidence and what would change it. Do not invent age/income/nationality to make
it appear researched. A small qualitative sample can refine hypotheses without
estimating how many people share them.

## Choose a promise and a boundary

Write: for [need/context], this channel offers [repeatable value] through [distinct
voice/format], unlike [actual alternative], supported by [capability/evidence].
Choose what the channel will decline. Explain trade-offs: a narrow premise may
improve clarity while reducing addressable topics; broad relatability may flatten
the author's local voice. “Relatable, high quality content” cannot guide a choice.

Compare two or three feasible positions using goal alignment, audience evidence,
distinctiveness, creative fit, resource needs and testability. Include “retain
current position” when evidence is weak. No market-size or share estimate without
the relevant data and denominator. A reference matrix records source/example,
date/selection, observable function, difference and an original response.

## Content-market fit and conflicting signals

Treat fit as a continuing hypothesis about a repeatable promise meeting a need
within sustainable constraints. Seek convergence: repeated consumption/returning
cohorts, unsolicited descriptions of the intended value, feasible production and
goal-relevant outcomes across several comparable releases. No universal threshold
or number of episodes proves fit. Predeclare the pilot's budget and decision date.

If vocal comments request darker humor but repeat viewers favor gentle episodes,
separate commenter vs viewer cohorts and check exposure/source/format. Do not
decide “the audience wants darkness.” Offer a bounded opt-in concept test or a
separate clearly labeled pilot, preserve current tone pending stronger evidence,
and log disconfirming comments. If there is no return/cohort data, say so.

Good: “Hypothesis: a recurring low-dialogue office comedy offers a quick sense
of recognition. Test comprehension and voluntary return.”
Counterexample: “Research proves all office workers want 20-second videos.”
The latter fabricates population, precision and duration preference.
