# Editorial portfolio and opportunity choices

P01/P07/P08/P12 in [notes](../research/notes.md). Portfolio allocations,
scorecards and lifecycle labels are local heuristics; negotiate them per project.

## Pillars become repeatable audience promises

A pillar joins audience need, channel point of view, repeatable series engine,
production cost and a learning question. “Office life” is a topic; “tiny work
frustrations resolved through kindness” gives a series boundary. Formats specify
recurring setup/variation/payoff and entry context without writing plots.
Track canon, protected reveals, episode order and how a new viewer can enter.
Do not spoil a serial mystery merely to make every clip standalone.

Choose genre, length and platform for the payoff and viewing context: short form
can test a self-contained situation economically, while long form can support
accumulation and character change at higher production/attention cost. A short
clip's success does not prove demand for a long episode. Propose a bridge test
and measure its own audience journey. Runtime is a target until implemented.

## Evergreen, topical, seasonal

Evergreen serves recurring needs and can compound a usable library; topical
content trades shelf life and lead time for timely relevance. Seasonal ideas
require locale, calendar/event dates, preparation lead time and prior comparable
periods; “holiday season” is not global. Reuse an evergreen series engine with a
timely context only when the original voice and payoff survive.

For trend lifecycle, inspect observations at comparable dates/windows: emerging,
growing, crowded, declining or unknown are reasoned labels, not official states.
Ask whether the idea will still matter when production finishes, what new value
the channel contributes, and whether licensing/cultural risk exceeds the option
value. A single screenshot or bot-like repost burst stays unverified. Don't
manufacture a growth chart or extrapolate Google Trends 100 into market size.

## Prioritize a backlog, then brief the owner

Use an ordinal scorecard only when comparison helps: goal fit, need evidence,
distinctive contribution, repeatability, feasibility and learning value (0–2 each,
with one sentence/evidence per rating). Sum is a discussion aid, **not** a success
probability. Unknown evidence gets “unknown,” not a fabricated midpoint. Separate
confidence/cost; vary weights or compare without scores when rank is unstable.
Rights, deception, incompatible locks and unapproved spend are gates, not offset
by points. Include opportunity cost and stop/park choices.

For a winning opportunity produce: audience/job, insight and evidence strength,
communication goal, truthful promise/payoff, genre/format/length/platform/locale
options, protected canon/reveals, accessibility, budget assumptions, acceptance
and experiment question. Director chooses creative interpretation; Storyteller
develops premise; craft owners implement. A list of image prompts is insufficient.

## Sustainable calendar

Compute capacity from actual or labeled estimated hours: development + assets +
animation + audio/edit + packaging + accessibility/localization + review + buffer.
An 8-hour weekly budget and a 6-hour base episode plus 3-hour localization/review
does not support a weekly release; reduce scope or cadence. Preserve recovery
time, buffer and creative experiments. No platform-independent best posting hour
or “daily posting unlocks the algorithm.” S05 provides YouTube-specific official
guidance, not a rule for TikTok/Instagram.

Calendar rows need episode/variant/version, intended region/locale/platform,
proposed local time and timezone, dependencies/owners, rights/policy recheck,
human release authorization state, workload and feedback date. Do not imply
scheduled posts exist. Recut/repurpose for a new context with its own promise,
caption/framing/music rights and continuity check; avoid multiplying production
effort across platforms before testing the channel's core value.
