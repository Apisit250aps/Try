# Distribution, localization, policy and business

P05/P07/P09/P11/P12 in [notes](../research/notes.md). This is a planning guide.
Platform guidance is a dated first-party statement, not independent proof of
ranking or a permanent specification. Inspect current official rules and the
authorized account's actual feature availability before action.

Do not simply duplicate a watermarked/poorly cropped version or assume removing
a watermark guarantees reach. Brief Editor on necessary reframing and captions,
Sound Designer on native audio rights and language versions, Artist on cover
assets. Compare costs with expected learning; use a clean approved master and
track every derivative back to episode/cut version. Packaging cannot reveal an
unapproved ending or promise an event absent from the actual cut.

## Accessibility and global audiences

“Global” is a goal, not a homogeneous segment. Select pilot locales using actual
evidence or label the choice provisional. Record language/script, region, cultural
context, viewing circumstance and review capability. Preserve names, pronouns,
relationships, jokes and canonical facts in a glossary; review idioms, gestures,
symbols, holidays and localized title promises with an appropriate speaker.
Back-translation is a check, not proof of cultural fit. Don't erase local voice
on a speculative claim that international audiences dislike it.

Captions include essential non-speech sound and speaker information as needed;
subtitles translating dialogue alone may omit access-critical meaning. Plan
description/transcripts for visual information (P09; S29). Check readability,
line breaks, contrast, UI overlap and actual timing on
the delivered size with Editor. Keep caption language and audio language separate.
Dubbing needs translation/performance/pronunciation and consent/voice-rights
checks with Screenwriter/Sound Designer; AI dubbing isn't automatically verified.
Silent comedy still needs access to sound cues/visual-only causal information.

Use a localization brief with source and target versions, protected reveals,
glossary, adapted vs literal choices, cultural reviewer/status, audio/caption
assets, timing changes, platform/region, rights, budget and acceptance. Unknown
native-language review remains a limitation. Local publishing time needs timezone
and date/DST; absent audience-activity evidence, call it an operational hypothesis,
not the best hour. Record equal post-age windows for learning across timezones.

## Rights, policy, disclosure and release

Create a platform/region/date checklist tied to the exact variant: originality,
source/music/voice/likeness/license scope, permissions, branded-content disclosure,
community/recommendation/advertiser suitability, AI role and required labels,
audience/age designation, and human release authorization. Check kid-directed
audiences from actual content/intent and rules; animation alone is insufficient.
Community compliance, recommendation eligibility, monetization and brand
suitability are different decisions. A label does not clear prohibited content
or third-party rights.

S06 gives YouTube disclosure examples; evaluate actual image/audio alterations,
not just “cartoon.” S12 and S16 show TikTok/Meta transparency approaches but are
not exhaustive current policies. Open live official help/community rules for the
actual use; if blocked, record the gap and leave dependent release clearance
pending. Do not turn indexed snippets into a verified policy certificate or strip
provenance metadata to evade labeling. Use a truthful disclosure proposal while
waiting for the human/platform check. No claim here certifies legal clearance.

## Monetization and sustainability

First ask which business goal: platform revenue, sponsorship, commissions,
membership, product or simply sustainable production. Requirements vary by
program, account, age, region and date. S07 establishes regional availability as
one YPP dependency; it does not establish this channel is eligible. Without a
specified region/current program/account evidence, provide a verification plan
and leave eligibility unknown. Do not freeze numerical thresholds in this skill.
TikTok Rewards and Instagram eligibility were not verified in this batch.

Budget production, localization, review, licensing, distribution and opportunity
cost. If asked for revenue, use user-supplied or explicitly hypothetical low/base/
high assumptions: eligible monetized units / 1,000 × assumed net revenue per
1,000, less specified costs. Separate gross from net, currency/time, platform
CPM (advertiser cost) from creator RPM (platform-defined revenue denominator),
fill/eligibility/attribution assumptions, and sponsor scenarios. Don't apply a
made-up RPM to all public views. Scenarios are sensitivity analysis, not forecasts
or guaranteed income. Prefer durable audience trust and feasible production over
unsustainable volume or policy-risk growth.
