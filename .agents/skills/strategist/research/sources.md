# Consulted source registry

Access date for every consulted entry: **2026-11-26 (Asia/Bangkok)**. Access is
to the named sections, not a claim to have read an entire site, book or paper.
Help pages without visible publication/update dates are marked **date not shown**;
copyright/crawl dates are not publication dates. Sources describe definitions,
methods and mechanisms; no channel, trend dataset or private analytics was supplied.
Claim → source mapping is in [notes](notes.md). Official advice is first-party
guidance, not an independently replicated ranking claim. Links below were opened;
where HTML extraction was blocked, the access log says so explicitly.

## Audience and communication research

### S18
George Loewenstein, **The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation**.
Psychological Bulletin 116(1), 75–98, July 1994; DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75.
[Author's university-hosted scan](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf).
Peer-reviewed theoretical review. Read scope: scan pp.75 and 87 (PDF pages 1/13),
not the whole review. Supports the perceived information-gap account and its
conditions. Scope: explanatory theory, not tested Shorts hook duration or guaranteed
curiosity. Scan needed visual inspection; no text extraction available.

### S19
Melanie C. Green & Timothy C. Brock, **The Role of Transportation in the Persuasiveness of Public Narratives**.
JPSP 79(5), 701–721, November 2000; DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701.
[University metadata/abstract](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/the-role-of-transportation-in-the-persuasiveness-of-public-narrat/),
[paper scan](https://www.communicationcache.com/uploads/1/0/8/8/10887248/the_role_of_transportation_in_the_persuasiveness_of_public_narratives.pdf).
Peer-reviewed empirical work. Read: abstract, Experiment 1 discussion and Experiment
4 rationale. Supports transportation as an absorption/belief mechanism; early
correlational analyses and later manipulations differ. Scope: reading studies,
not direct evidence of short-video retention or global animation preference.

### S20
Jonah Berger & Katherine L. Milkman, **What Makes Online Content Viral?**
Journal of Marketing Research 49(2), 192–205, April 2012; DOI 10.1509/jmr.10.0353.
[University-hosted paper](https://cssh.northeastern.edu/pandemic-teaching-initiative/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2020/09/What-Makes-Online-Content-Viral.pdf).
Peer-reviewed field analysis plus experiments. Read: abstract/Study 1 framing,
Study 3 discussion/general discussion. Supports arousal as one sharing mechanism.
Scope: news emailing/laboratory transmission, not modern feed algorithms or a
prescription to provoke anger; no universal effect size transferred.

### S21
Annie Lang, **The limited capacity model of mediated message processing**.
Journal of Communication 50(1), 46–70 (2000), DOI 10.1111/j.1460-2466.2000.tb02833.x.
[Publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1460-2466.2000.tb02833.x).
Peer-reviewed model; **abstract only read**. Publisher displays online-first date
2006-01-10 while title/issue identifies 2000; record both, don't silently replace
the issue year. Scope: television-processing model; no fixed attention span or
modern social-video pacing prescription established by this access.

## Research, strategy and experimentation methods

### S22
Widad Machmouchi, Somit Gupta, Ruhan Zhang & Aleksander Fabijan / Microsoft ExP,
**Patterns of Trustworthy Experimentation: Pre-Experiment Stage**.
[Primary practitioner research article](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-pre-experiment-stage/).
Published 2020-07-31. Read: hypothesis/metrics, randomization unit, rollout caveats.
Supports falsifiable outcomes, guardrails, power and assignment choices. Scope:
product experimentation with controlled exposure; doesn't validate organic post
comparisons as randomized trials.

### S23
Pew Research Center, **Writing Survey Questions**.
[Research-methods guidance](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions/).
Date not shown; institutional author. Read: development/pretesting, change over
time, wording/order. Supports careful recruitment questions and consistent measures.
Scope: questionnaire methodology; applying it to comments/interviews is local
adaptation, not evidence those samples represent an audience.

### S24
Courtney Kennedy / Pew Research Center, **What are nonprobability surveys?**
[Methods explainer](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2018/08/06/what-are-nonprobability-surveys/).
Published 2018-08-06. Read: introductory text (not linked video).
Supports distinguishing opt-in from probability sampling. Scope: explanation,
not a completed audience study or margin-of-error formula for convenience samples.

### S25
Clayton Christensen Institute, **Jobs to Be Done Theory**.
[Primary framework explanation](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).
Date not shown; institutional author. Read: definition and functional/social/
emotional circumstances. Supports a needs/context lens for segmentation.
Scope: innovation/practitioner framework, not an experimentally validated persona
for this series; case-study claims not imported as audience statistics.

### S26
Widad Machmouchi, Somit Gupta & Ruhan Zhang / Microsoft ExP,
**Patterns of Trustworthy Experimentation: During-Experiment Stage**.
[Primary practitioner research article](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-during-experiment-stage/).
Published 2021-01-25. Read: metric taxonomy, monitoring/peeking, sample-ratio
mismatch. Supports guardrails/data quality and planned statistical analysis.
Scope: controlled product experiments; their example duration is not a universal
content-test duration and repeated checking needs appropriate methods.

### S28
Katja Hofmann, Lihong Li & Filip Radlinski, **Online Evaluation for Information Retrieval**.
Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval 10(1), 1–117 (2016), DOI 10.1561/1500000051.
[Author/organization-hosted survey](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/ftir-online-evaluation-final-journal.pdf).
Scholarly methods survey. Read: abstract and §2.2.2–2.2.3, printed pp.15–16;
not all 117 pages. Supports design-dependent sample size and independent units.
Scope: information retrieval; social diffusion/repeated views need additional
design assumptions, not a canned sample count.

### S29
W3C WAI, editor Shawn Lawton Henry, **Making Audio and Video Media Accessible**.
[Institutional accessibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/).
First published September 2019; updated 2024-09-17. Read: planning, description,
captions/subtitles and transcript summary. Supports planning sensory access early.
Scope: overview/standards guidance, not proof of accessibility of an unproduced
video or machine translation quality.

### S30
Content Marketing Institute, **Developing a Content Marketing Strategy**.
[Primary practitioner guidance](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/developing-a-content-marketing-strategy).
Date not shown; institutional author. Read: strategy vs plan and components.
Supports separating goals/audience/value from tactical execution and governance.
Scope: marketing practice, not peer-reviewed causal proof that documenting a
strategy improves performance; no survey effectiveness statistics adopted.
