# Research and no-data workflow

Use when a decision depends on audience behavior, trends, competition or current
platform rules. Principles P01, P05–P08, P11; [source map](../research/notes.md).

1. Write the decision before searching: “Should this series test an office-relief
   pillar in Thai and English?” Separate audience demand, creative feasibility
   and business return. Define geography/language/population/window and what
   observation could reverse the recommendation.
2. Inventory tools and permissions: can browse current public text, inspect an
   export, read video/audio, query an authorized connector, or only reason from
   the prompt? List unavailable modalities. Readable documentation does not
   establish live feature access. No scraper, analytics API or trend feed is
   provisioned by this skill.
3. In research mode, open original pages, inspect relevant sections and dates,
   record final URLs/locators and access limits. Search snippets are leads,
   not consulted full sources. For policy/metrics use current official pages;
   for behavioral mechanisms prefer accessible peer-reviewed originals/reviews;
   for workflow use practitioner sources with that label. Never claim an
   inaccessible textbook was read. Capture a short supporting paraphrase and
   scope/limitation, not copyrighted page dumps.
4. Build a claim ledger: evidence ID → scoped observation → inference → decision.
   Check an important claim against a genuinely different source or method.
   A repost citing the same report is not corroboration. Check conflicting date,
   locale, denominator, paid/organic and study design before averaging claims.
   Leave unresolved contradictions visible and reduce confidence.
5. For a trend, log exact query/topic, platform/search surface, region, locale,
   category, filters, observation/retrieval dates, baseline and comparable window,
   metric definition, examples and representativeness. Distinguish accumulated
   popularity from growth rate and original posts from copies. Google Trends
   index is relative sampled search interest, not people or purchase demand
   (S17). No accessible observations means “suggested idea; trend unverified.”
6. For audience research, sample by viewing circumstance, new/returning behavior
   and locale, including quiet viewers/non-fans where feasible. Record recruitment,
   inclusion, sample size, missing groups and exact question wording. Ask about
   a recent concrete viewing choice, then unmet need; avoid “Would you love our
   funny series?” Pretest translation and questions. Convenience comments/polls
   cannot establish population prevalence (S23, S24).
7. For competitor/reference analysis, define selection criteria, compare ordinary
   as well as successful items, same format/post age and public evidence scope.
   Log observable promise, format, audience context, distinctiveness and gaps.
   Public counts cannot reveal private CTR, retention or revenue. Translate a
   functional lesson into an original alternative, never copy scenes, wording,
   identity or thumbnails. Check survivor/selection bias.
8. End with sourced facts, unresolved questions, recommendation confidence,
   alternatives/cost, and the cheapest useful next observation. Source freshness
   is risk-based: verify policy/eligibility/features before action; verify trend
   signals near the production/release decision; revisit stable research when
   new evidence or a different population challenges transfer. No universal TTL.

## No-data fallback

Declare unavailable account/trend/market data. Produce a useful provisional
positioning, hypothesis personas, a small sustainable pilot, decision criteria
and a minimal data collection plan. Do not hide a guessed persona behind
demographic detail or turn an interview target into a completed study. Label
expected direction, not an unsupported numeric uplift. If browsing is absent,
do not give current platform eligibility or “today's trends”; link a verification
task and continue with evergreen strategy.
